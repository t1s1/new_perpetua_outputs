## 1. What this project is

A simulated playground swing (MuJoCo) with a rider that has two actuated mechanisms — a LEAN hinge (like a hip, connecting the rope/chain to the seat) and a KNEE hinge (rotating a lower-leg mass to shift the center of gravity) — learns via reinforcement learning (PPO, Stable-Baselines3) to pump itself up from a near-dead stop, with no hand-coded pumping logic. `manual_pump.py` contains a hand-coded reference controller used only for comparison/sanity-checking, not as part of the trained pipeline.

Eventual goal: make the simulation more physically realistic, then attempt transfer to a real mechanism. See **Roadmap** at the bottom — currently on step 1 of that list.

## 4. How the pieces fit together

- `train.py` always resumes from `swing_policy.zip` (the raw, unprotected "wherever training last ended" state) — **not** from `best_model/`.
- `best_model/best_model.zip` is a separately-tracked "best seen so far" checkpoint, gated by `EvalCallback` (5-episode internal evaluation, matching `evaluate.py`'s own rigor as of the fix in section 5.3).
- `evaluate.py` and `auto_train.py` both default to checking `best_model/best_model`, not `swing_policy.zip`.
- `watch_policy.py` also defaults to `best_model/best_model` (this was a real source of confusion earlier — it originally defaulted to `swing_policy`, which could silently be a worse/different checkpoint than what was being evaluated).
- **Promoting a checkpoint** (via `promote_checkpoint.py`) means copying it into *both* `swing_policy.zip` and `best_model/best_model.zip` — copying to only one was a real mistake made earlier in this project, since each path is read by different scripts.

## 5. Key lessons learned

### 5.1 Cold start needs a seed kick
Episodes don't start from exact dead rest — `reset()` applies a small randomized angular velocity kick. An earlier version started from exact rest and asked the policy to invent its own symmetry-breaking motion; it instead learned to rely on its own per-step exploration noise as a substitute, which showed up as a large gap between deterministic and stochastic evaluation reward. This is also why the very start of an episode looks "noisy/rocking" before the pump settles — that's the policy reacting to and amplifying the seed kick, not a bug.

### 5.2 Episode length and reward shaping
`TARGET_ANGLE_DEG=80` in `swing_env.py` caps the height reward (no benefit to swinging higher) and a gentle quadratic penalty (`OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT=0.02`, starting `OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG=10` past target) discourages overshoot — needed because nothing about the rigid-rod pendulum physically discourages high angles the way a real slack chain would. `episode_seconds=240` gives enough time for a policy to actually bank reward for holding near target, not just touch it once as the episode truncates. `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` is currently `0.0` (off) — it's staged to be turned on *after* a working policy exists, to smooth jittery actions without discouraging real phase-switch transitions during initial learning.

### 5.3 EvalCallback's own gate needs the same rigor as reporting
`EvalCallback`'s internal "is this a new best" comparison originally used only 3 episodes — the same small-sample noise problem `evaluate.py`'s 5-episode averaging exists to avoid, but the fix was never applied to the actual gate deciding what gets saved. A collapsing policy could get lucky on 3 episodes and permanently overwrite a genuinely better `best_model.zip`. Fixed by raising `N_EVAL_EPISODES_FOR_BEST` to 5. Even at 5 episodes, this gate isn't perfectly reliable given how volatile this policy can be (see 5.5) — `best_model.zip` has been observed to still get overwritten with something worse on occasion.

### 5.4 `auto_train.py`'s six stopping conditions
Built up incrementally after real failures, each one motivated by something that actually happened and initially went undetected:
- **Target reached**: requires `CONSECUTIVE_HITS_REQUIRED=3` rounds *in a row*, each independently passing angle ≥ `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` (75), overshoot ≤ `TARGET_OVERSHOOT_MAX` (0.1), and spread ≤ `SPREAD_MAX_DEG` (15) — a single good round can be a lucky read, so this requires repeated, low-variance evidence.
- **Stall**: `STALL_ROUNDS=5` consecutive rounds swinging by less than `STALL_IMPROVEMENT_DEG=1.0` — catches a FLAT plateau, nothing else.
- **Decline**: `DECLINE_WINDOW=4` consecutive rounds monotonically non-increasing with a total drop ≥ `DECLINE_MIN_DROP_DEG=5.0` — catches a *gradual* multi-round slide, which the stall check explicitly can't (a decline is a big swing, just the wrong direction).
- **Cliff**: a single round dropping to less than `CLIFF_DROP_FRACTION=0.5` of the immediately preceding round (when that prior round was ≥ `CLIFF_MIN_PRIOR_DEG=20`) — catches a *sudden* one-round collapse, which the decline check can miss if the round right before the cliff happened to be an increase (breaks the "monotonic" requirement).
- **Erratic**: a single round whose own spread exceeds `ERRATIC_SPREAD_MAX_DEG` (90) or overshoot exceeds `ERRATIC_OVERSHOOT_MAX` (400) — independent of the mean angle entirely. Motivated by a real round where the mean (73.04°) looked like ordinary not-yet-converged noise, but that round's own 5 episodes ranged from 10.4° to 122.3° with an overshoot penalty of 1175.63 — none of the other five checks look at anything but the mean, so all of them missed it. This is what the other checks structurally can't see: a round can look fine on average while actually alternating between near-collapse and wild overshoot underneath.
- **Round cap**: `MAX_ROUNDS=3` — deliberately small so a check-in is a short, boundable task rather than requiring someone to babysit a long unattended run.

All of these read from the *full persisted history* in `evaluate_log.csv`, filtered to rows where `model_path == "best_model/best_model"` (so ad hoc manual checks against other checkpoints don't pollute the streak/decline/stall tracking), not just the current invocation's own rounds — this matters because with `MAX_ROUNDS=3`, a real trend will often only become visible across several separate check-ins.

**Known limitation:** these checks compare consecutive rows as if they're one continuous trend. A deliberate checkpoint promotion breaks that assumption (the "before" and "after" aren't really comparable) — `promote_checkpoint.py` handles this by archiving and resetting `evaluate_log.csv` on every promotion, so the checks aren't confused by comparing across a deliberate reset.

### 5.5 The collapse-and-recovery saga, and what actually helped
Training repeatedly showed a pattern of long good stretches suddenly collapsing to near-zero performance, sometimes recovering, sometimes not for millions of steps. Investigated one variable at a time:

- **Reward normalization** (`VecNormalize(norm_reward=True)`): turned off. Hypothesis was a feedback loop — reward variance spikes as a policy gets inconsistent, the normalizer shrinks the training signal in response, gradient information gets noisier right when consistency matters most. Helped, not sufficient alone.
- **Gamma**: reduced 0.9995 → 0.999 → 0.997 (shorter value-function horizon, less variance-prone estimation). **Bug found**: `PPO.load()` restores hyperparameters baked into the saved checkpoint unless explicitly overridden — the resuming code path wasn't passing `gamma=` to `.load()`, so every resumed run silently kept whichever gamma the loaded checkpoint originally had. The gamma change likely never took effect until this was fixed (now explicitly passed on every `.load()` call).
- **`target_kl`**: added at 0.03, after TensorBoard showed `train/approx_kl` spiking to 0.4+ repeatedly (healthy PPO is typically ~0.01–0.05) and `train/clip_fraction` chronically elevated at 15–28% for most of training, not just during collapses. SB3 defaults `target_kl` to `None` — no circuit breaker existed to stop a batch's gradient epochs early once KL divergence got too large. This is the most directly-evidenced fix of the three (found by reading actual training metrics, not inferring from before/after eval snapshots).

**Working hypothesis for the remaining bimodality** (even after all three fixes): the policy's ability to pump appears to depend on hitting precise phase-timing — pushing at the right moment amplifies the swing, pushing even slightly out of phase actively cancels it. Evidence: checkpoint-to-checkpoint results are almost perfectly binary (either ~65–77° or ~2–4°, almost nothing between), and even a single checkpoint's own 5 evaluation episodes can show a wide spread — consistent with the *same* weights landing on different sides of a phase-lock boundary depending on the specific random seed kick each episode receives. This resonates with real-world swing-pumping intuition (timing matters a lot) and may be somewhat inherent to the task rather than something a further hyperparameter search would fully eliminate.

**Practical implication**: rather than continuing to chase 3 consecutive fully-converged `auto_train.py` rounds, the project settled on finding one well-supported checkpoint (backed by a long streak of consecutive good readings, not an isolated lucky spike) and using that as the deliverable for this phase.

### 5.5a Recurring wild overshoot was a fixable reward-shaping gap, not phase-lock
Distinct from the phase-lock bimodality above: on the knee/lean-fixed model, TWO independent training runs — one from scratch, one resumed from a verified-good ~76° checkpoint (`swing_ppo_10458080_steps`) — both drifted into sustained 100-140°+ overshoot within a few million further steps, with per-episode overshoot penalties reaching 1000-2800 under the old `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT=0.02`. Recurring the same way from two different starting points is what distinguishes this from the phase-lock story: that pattern is checkpoint-to-checkpoint bimodal (good OR collapsed), this one is a directional drift toward a specific failure mode (overshoot specifically, not just instability generally), which pointed at the reward shaping itself rather than something inherent to the task. Fixed by raising `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` to `0.08` (4x) — a reasoned first step given how far off the old value clearly was, not a precisely-tuned value. Plausible mechanism: the reward landscape between `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` (80) and `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG + OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG` (90) is flat (no benefit to reaching 80, no cost until 90), giving a "coast" zone with no corrective gradient right where the resonant pumping dynamics make it easy to keep drifting higher. This is a reward-function-only change — doesn't touch the model's observation/action space or joint structure, so no `archive_old_model.py` reset needed; safe to resume the existing promoted checkpoint under the new weight, same pattern already used for staging `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT`.

### 5.5a-ii The 0.08 fix wasn't enough either — raised to 0.3
Training resumed from the promoted `swing_ppo_10458080_steps` checkpoint (~57 deg) drifted deeper into overshoot on every subsequent check-in rather than correcting, under the `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT=0.08` value from 5.5a: 84 deg/857 penalty, then 108.7 deg/4389 penalty, then 95.6 deg/2105 penalty with a 62.6 deg spread. Same resonant coast-and-amplify pattern as the original 0.02 fix, just slower to show up — third occurrence of this failure mode across two different weight values, not a fluke. Raised to `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT=0.3` (a bigger jump than the last 4x bump, deliberately, to get an unambiguous read either way).

**What "too high" would look like, since that's a real risk in the other direction**: a policy that converges cleanly (low episode-to-episode spread, near-zero overshoot penalty) but plateaus noticeably *below* target instead of approaching it — distinct from anything seen in this project so far, since the pattern to date has been closer to binary (~65-77 deg or near-total collapse) rather than a deliberately timid middle ground. None of `auto_train.py`'s six stopping conditions (5.4) distinguish this from an ordinary early-training stall — a `stall` message means "angle stopped moving," not why — so this needs a human glance at the actual angle level and overshoot penalty when a stall fires, not just the fact that it fired. Watch for this after resuming training under 0.3; back the weight down if it shows up, and if instead the same high-overshoot/high-spread instability persists, 0.3 wasn't the fix and the problem is more likely something other than this weight.

### 5.5a-iii Found a mechanism, not just a symptom: unbounded value-function updates from outlier returns
Rather than guess at another lever after 5.5a-ii, read `tb_logs/PPO_1/` directly for the training run covering the `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT=0.3` rounds. Found a concrete, reproducible signature right at the step of a catastrophic-overshoot checkpoint (`swing_ppo_18468736_steps`: reward -23124, angle 115.1 deg): `train/value_loss` spiked to 2943.9 (typical range 0.6-25), `train/explained_variance` crashed from its usual 0.91-0.99 down to 0.55-0.65, and `train/approx_kl` spiked to 0.037 (above `TARGET_KL=0.03`) — all at the same step.

Plausible mechanism: PPO's value function trains with plain MSE loss, which is very sensitive to outlier targets. A rare, extreme-magnitude overshoot episode produces a wildly outlying return; one such episode in a rollout batch can blow up that batch's value estimates. Since GAE advantages are computed *from* the value function, a corrupted value estimate feeds a corrupted advantage into the policy update — a believable proximate cause for at least some of this project's "sudden single-round collapse" pattern (the shape `auto_train.py`'s cliff check exists to catch), distinct from the phase-lock bimodality hypothesis in 5.5 above.

**First attempt (tried, didn't work, reverted): `clip_range_vf`.** Checked `train.py`'s `PPO(...)` construction, found only `gamma`/`target_kl` were ever overridden, so `clip_range_vf` sat at SB3's default of `None`. Set it to `0.2` (by analogy to the policy's own `clip_range=0.2`) on both the fresh-start and resumed `PPO.load()` paths. Next training round: `train/value_loss` spiked to 1,269,687 with it active — *worse* than the 2,943.9 that motivated the fix, and `explained_variance` went negative (-2.60). Wrong diagnosis of the mechanism: `clip_range_vf` bounds how far the *new* value prediction can move from the *old* prediction within a batch's epochs — it does nothing to bound the loss against the actual return target, so a wildly outlying return (this round: -65,289 from a 123.8 deg excursion) still dominates the MSE regardless of how clamped the prediction is. Also just numerically mismatched to this environment regardless of that: `clip_range_vf` is an absolute bound in raw return-units, not a ratio like the policy's `clip_range` it was copied from, and this environment's returns run into the thousands — `0.2` was close to meaningless at that scale either way. Reverted both the constant and its use in both `PPO(...)` and `PPO.load()`.

**Second attempt (tried, insufficient, superseded): flat per-step reward cap.** The thing that needed bounding was the regression target itself, not the value function's reaction to it — same idea as the reward clipping Atari-style DQN/PPO papers use for this exact failure mode. Added `OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP = 50.0` in `swing_env.py`, applied to the per-step overshoot penalty before it's subtracted from reward. Reasoned at the time: roughly 30x the max per-step height reward (~1.65), so overshoot stays clearly the dominant per-step cost whenever it happens, while preventing the quadratic tail from producing five- and six-figure episode returns.

Next training round showed this was necessary but not sufficient: worst single-episode return went from -65,289 (uncapped) to -26,742 (per-step cap active) — real improvement, same order-of-magnitude problem. Reason: a flat per-step cap bounds the *instantaneous* penalty, but nothing bounds how many consecutive steps it can accumulate over. With a ~12,000-step episode, SUSTAINED overshoot (not a single spike) can still rack up tens of thousands in cumulative penalty even with every individual step capped at 50 — per-step and per-episode are genuinely different constraints once episodes run this long, and only the per-episode total is what actually matters for value-function stability.

**Actual fix: an episode-level overshoot penalty budget.** Replaced the flat per-step cap with `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP = 500.0` and a `self.cumulative_overshoot_penalty` accumulator (reset in `reset()`, not part of the observation — it's reward-shaping bookkeeping, not physical state). Each step's applied penalty is `min(raw_penalty, cap - spent_so_far)`, floored at 0 — early steps of an excursion are penalized at full strength (preserving the sharp gradient signal right at the margin), but once the episode has already accumulated enough penalty to clearly mark it as bad, further sustained overshoot stops adding more. That's what actually bounds the worst-case EPISODE return regardless of excursion length, which a per-step-only cap structurally can't do. 500.0 is reasoned, not precisely tuned: clearly negative enough to distinguish a bad episode from a good one (a few hundred to a few thousand, positive) without being anywhere near the five- or six-figure range that was corrupting the value function.

Also reframes `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` again: raising it to 0.3 doesn't just fail to prevent overshoot outright (confirmed — 115+ deg excursions still happened) — it also widened the tail of the return distribution exactly when overshoot occurred, which is what fed this value-function-blowup mechanism in the first place. Worth revisiting `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` once the episode-level cap has had a chance to run, now judging it against this more precise mechanism instead of the "does it look too timid" framing from 5.5a-ii.

### 5.5b `scan_checkpoints.py`'s streak detector needed a ceiling, not just a floor
`find_best_streak()` (5.6-era addition) originally only checked `angle >= STREAK_GOOD_MIN_DEG` — no upper bound. On the knee/lean-fixed model this produced a real bad recommendation: an "18-checkpoint streak" that was actually mostly 85-124°, badly overshooting the 80° target (overshoot penalty starts at 90° per `swing_env.py`). A checkpoint that consistently reads way past target isn't better, it's poorly calibrated and getting penalized hard for it — the opposite of converged, even though the raw angle number looks impressive. Fixed with a band (`STREAK_GOOD_MIN_DEG=55`, `STREAK_GOOD_MAX_DEG=95`) instead of a floor. The same root cause was also quietly distorting the older collapse-point heuristic: its `best_so_far` reference tracked the raw running max, so an overshoot spike (e.g. 123.6°) could inflate the reference point and make an unrelated, perfectly ordinary later reading look like a bigger collapse than it was. Fixed the same way — `best_so_far` now only updates from angles within the good band.

### 5.5c `scan_checkpoints.py`'s incremental cache went stale after a rescue promotion
"Already scanned" used to mean nothing more than "this step number appears in `checkpoint_scan.csv`" — true within one continuous lineage, but false the moment training resumes from an earlier PROMOTED checkpoint (the normal rescue workflow, section 5.6). `train.py`'s step counter continues from the loaded checkpoint's own count, not from wherever the run being rescued from had reached, so `CheckpointCallback`'s next saves reuse step numbers (and filenames) the original run already had, silently overwriting those files on disk with unrelated weights. This actually happened: a rescue promotion back to a ~10.4M-step checkpoint, followed by more training, overwrote several already-scanned files in the 10.4M–11.4M range, and a scan afterward reported "0 new" — technically true by step number, misleading in practice, since the content had changed. Fixed by tracking each row's file mtime at scan time and comparing against the checkpoint's current mtime on disk: a file that's changed since it was last scanned gets rescanned regardless of whether its step number was already seen. Also added the same `EXPECTED_HEADER` self-healing `evaluate.py` already had (needed since this added a new `mtime` column to the CSV schema).

### 5.6 Checkpoint provenance is imperfect
Multiple resumed/promoted training lineages ended up sharing overlapping step-number ranges in `checkpoints/` (since `CheckpointCallback` numbers files by the loaded model's internal step counter, which can collide with an earlier lineage's numbering after a checkpoint swap). This makes some historical checkpoint-scan data hard to fully reconstruct into one clean timeline — doesn't block picking good checkpoints going forward, but worth knowing if old scan data looks inconsistent.

**Concretely, this showed up in the phase-3 (knee model) checkpoint scan**: reading `checkpoint_scan.csv` in plain chronological step order looked like rapid, erratic good/collapse alternation from ~11M steps onward. Grouping by `step % 50000` instead (different `train.py` resume sessions land on different offsets, since the internal step counter's phase relative to the 50k save interval shifts on every restart) revealed two of those "erratic" stretches were actually two separate resume-session lineages interleaved together — one almost entirely good, one almost entirely collapsed. `scan_checkpoints.py` now does this grouping automatically (`find_best_streak()`) and prints the longest well-supported streak plus ready-to-run `evaluate.py`/`promote_checkpoint.py` commands, instead of requiring this to be untangled by hand each time.

### 5.7 Translating the hand-drawn diagram into MJCF
The diagram showed the swing at two points in its arc (same rig, not two designs), with a key distinguishing rope/chain (thick line), servo hinges (filled), free hinges (open), and body mass (diamond). Two genuinely ambiguous points got resolved through back-and-forth rather than guessed at:
- **Rope/chain fidelity**: a true flexible multi-link chain was considered but set aside — the user was explicit this "isn't a hill to die on," and a single rigid link keeps the swing's core dynamics (already tuned once) unchanged while still reflecting the new LEAN/KNEE joint structure. Worth revisiting later if slack/whip behavior near horizontal ever becomes something the project needs to capture.
- **Arms**: the diagram's 3-link free-hinge arm chain (wrist/elbow/shoulder, hand welded to the rope) would create a closed kinematic loop with the seat's LEAN attachment — solvable with a MuJoCo equality constraint, but added complexity for a part of the rig the user confirmed has "no function" dynamically. Left out of the model entirely.
- **Lean asymmetry**: not caught on the first pass — found by re-examining the diagram after the user flagged it directly. The two swing-extreme figures show unequal lean magnitudes (bigger recline one way than the other), which the original symmetric `±45°` range couldn't represent. Refined from an initial `-25/+55°` visual read to `-5/+80°`, the user's own reasoned estimate of real hip range of motion (negative = forward lean, positive = backward recline) — not blocking on lab-grade precision, since it's easy to retune once there's a trained policy to look at. The less obvious part was that the *code*, not just the XML number, needed to change — `swing_env.py`'s action scaling and observation bounds both silently assumed symmetry (see section 2).
- **Torso/seat inversion**: also not caught on the first translation pass — the diagram's own key (rope/chain, servo hinge, free hinge, body mass) doesn't actually encode WHICH side of an attachment point a body's mass sits on, so this one slipped through both the initial translation and several rounds of review, and was only found when the user looked directly at the compiled model's structure rather than at the diagram again. `torso_geom` was hanging below `lean_hinge` (chain effectively attached near the head, body dangling down) instead of rising above it (chain attaches at the seat/hip, torso rises up) — see section 2 for the fix. Worth remembering as a general lesson: a translation can satisfy every explicit rule in a diagram's key and still get an unstated spatial relationship backwards, especially one as easy to eyeball-miss as "which side of the joint does the mass go on."

### 5.5a-iv Shape, not magnitude: OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG lowered to 0
After the episode-level penalty budget (5.5a-iii) and the `swing_hinge` range limit (5.8), the resonant overshoot drift from 5.5a recurred a fourth time, worse and faster than before: training resumed from a clean, verified, zero-overshoot promoted checkpoint (`swing_ppo_24313584_steps`, 65.67 deg mean, 0.00 overshoot) and within 3 rounds every single evaluated episode — deterministic and stochastic, all 10 — hit or came close to the full 500-point episode penalty budget, with individual episodes reaching 165 deg (repeatedly hitting the new joint limit, not just grazing it once).

Three mitigations in a row (weight 0.02→0.08→0.3, the episode budget, the joint limit) all address how HARD the eventual penalty hits or how far the arm can physically go — none of them touch WHEN the penalty starts. Looked at the actual shape of the height reward instead: `height = arm_length * (1 - cos(angle))`, so its gradient w.r.t. angle is `sin(angle)` — strictly increasing over the entire 0-80 deg range. The incentive to keep pumping gets steeper, not flatter, right up to the target, then `min(angle_deg, TARGET_ANGLE_DEG)` drops that gradient (sin(80) = 0.985, near its steepest possible value) discontinuously to zero exactly at 80 deg. With the old `OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG=10`, nothing then pushed back for a further 10 degrees — the policy was being encouraged to build maximum momentum right up to the cap, then given a stretch to coast on that momentum with zero corrective signal before any penalty existed. Driving analogy: flooring the accelerator right up to the point the brakes slam on, instead of letting up beforehand.

Lowered `OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG` to `0.0` — the corrective gradient now starts exactly where the height reward's own gradient was steepest and then vanished, instead of 10 degrees later. This is a distinct, complementary lever to the others: shape (when the penalty starts) rather than magnitude (how hard it hits once started) — doesn't touch `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`, the episode penalty budget, or the joint range limit.

### 5.5a-v Easing off the accelerator, not just adding brakes: tapered height reward
The margin fix (5.5a-iv) alone made the very next round *more* extreme, not less: one round's 5 episodes ranged from 8.8 deg (near-total collapse) to 165.4 deg (the joint limit), almost nothing near 80 itself — the widest, most bimodal single-round spread seen in this whole investigation. Genuinely unclear whether that was the margin change backfiring (removing all slack around the target turns it into a needle instead of a landing zone) or just more of the ongoing baseline volatility coinciding with it — three rounds isn't enough to tell, and it's not necessarily an either/or.

Either way, this addresses the other half of the "letting up on the accelerator before slamming the brakes" idea from 5.5a-iv: that fix controls when the *penalty* starts past target; this controls when the *reward* stops accelerating before it. `height = arm_length * (1 - cos(angle))` still has derivative `sin(angle)`, still increasing over the whole 0-80 deg range regardless of where the margin sits — the policy was still being handed maximum incentive to keep pumping harder right up to the moment reward capped out.

Added `HEIGHT_TAPER_START_DEG = 67.5` (midpoint of the requested 65-70 range, reasoned not tuned) and a new `_height_reward()` helper: true, unmodified physical height below that angle (doesn't touch normal low-angle dynamics at all), eased to a flat landing (zero slope, verified numerically) exactly at `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` above it, instead of arriving there at the curve's steepest point and dropping straight to zero. Continuous in value at the 67.5 deg boundary; not continuous in slope there (a small kink, not a fully smooth spline) — a pragmatic simplification, and a much smaller discontinuity than the one it replaces at 80 deg. Complementary to the margin fix, not a replacement for it — both now address the same underlying mechanism from opposite sides of the target.

### 5.8 `swing_hinge` had no range limit at all
Found via `scan_checkpoints.py`, not the diagram-translation pass in 5.7: a real deterministic-episode scan of `swing_ppo_24563584_steps` logged a peak angle of 574.2 deg — more than 1.5 full rotations around the pivot. Checked `swing_robot.xml`: `swing_hinge` was the only one of the three joints with no `range` attribute at all (`lean_hinge` and `knee_hinge` both have one). Nothing in the physics stopped the arm from spinning past horizontal and looping over the top given enough energy — a rigid, unconstrained MuJoCo hinge genuinely allows that; a real swing (even modeled as a rigid link per the design decision in 5.7) obviously cannot, since the chain/mount would go slack or hit a mechanical stop long before inverting over the pivot.

Added `range="-165 165"` (symmetric, matching how `swing_env.py` already treats the angle via `abs(angle)`). 165, not 180, leaves a small margin short of true vertical/inversion, while sitting comfortably above anything training has actually aimed for or organically explored — worst overshoot excursions logged elsewhere top out well under 150 deg, so this shouldn't interfere with ordinary (if erratic) training, only rule out the physically-nonsensical full-rotation case.

**Didn't run `archive_old_model.py` for this one.** Unlike the knee-swap (changed what an action physically does) or the torso/seat fix (changed the effective pendulum length and mass distribution — both in 5.7/section 2), this constraint sits entirely outside the region training has ever actually operated in. Nothing about the dynamics changes anywhere below 165 deg — only the previously-reachable-but-nonsensical extreme tail is now blocked. Existing checkpoints and `swing_policy.zip` should still be valid to resume from.

### 5.5a-vi Duration-based budget left a flat zone past exhaustion — added a peak-severity term

`EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP` (5.5a-iii) fixed the value-function blowup by making the per-step overshoot penalty exhaustible within an episode. That created a side effect nothing had addressed yet: once a bad episode's 500-point budget is spent, every further step past the margin costs nothing more — 81 deg and 165 deg (the hard joint limit added in 5.8) become reward-equivalent for the rest of that episode. Worked out the budget exhausts in roughly 15-20 steps once `overshoot_deg` reaches ~10 (`500 / (0.3 * 10**2)`), a tiny fraction of a ~12,000-step episode, so this "nothing left to lose" zone is reachable almost immediately in any episode that overshoots at all — not just a rare worst case.

Considered whether the two most recent changes (`OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG` → 0, 5.5a-iv; the height taper, 5.5a-v) caused this and should be rolled back. They don't: the flat-past-80-deg height cap and the exhaustible budget both predate them, so the flat zone was already structurally present. The height taper only reshapes the curve leading up to 80 deg and doesn't touch anything past it — unrelated to this mechanism. `OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG=0` is a plausible *amplifier* though, worth keeping in mind: starting the penalty clock at 80 instead of 90 means the budget depletes sooner in wall-clock terms for a given trajectory, which could make this zone easier to reach than it would've been at margin=10. Not reverting it for that alone, since it's solving a distinct, still-valid problem (pre-target momentum buildup) — but if the fix below doesn't fully resolve things, restoring a small margin is a reasonable next lever.

Added `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` (`swing_env.py`): tracks `self.peak_overshoot_deg`, the worst `overshoot_deg` reached so far in the episode (reset in `reset()`), and charges `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT * (new_peak**2 - old_peak**2)` only on the step that sets a new worst point. Deliberately not drawn from `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP`'s budget, so it can't be exhausted by sustained duration the way that term can — it's a one-time charge per new extreme, not a per-step cost. Bounded by construction: since 5.8 hard-caps the joint at 165 deg, the worst possible one-time charge is a known `0.2 * 85**2 = 1445`, clearly punishing without reintroducing the unbounded-regression-target problem 5.5a-iii was added to fix. A modest, quickly-corrected excursion to 90 deg only costs `0.2 * 10**2 = 20` by contrast. 0.2 is a reasoned starting point, not a tuned value — same caveat as the other weights in this saga.

### 5.5a-vii The flat height cap was itself the exploit — decayed it past target

`PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` shipped and training resumed. Next `evaluate.py` round: 4/5 deterministic episodes settled at 165.4-165.5 deg — pinned at the hard joint limit (5.8), not just briefly passing through it — and those episodes scored *higher* reward (3000-4884) than the one that stayed controlled near target (1337.6 at 96.5 deg peak). Direct evidence the problem was never just "overshoot isn't punished enough": the flat height cap (`min(angle_deg, TARGET_ANGLE_DEG)`, in place since the reward was first written) meant a rod wedged against the mechanical stop earned the *same* per-step height reward as hovering at 80 deg, with a lower action-rate cost besides, since holding against a hard stop needs no ongoing correction. `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP` and `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` are both deliberately bounded (to avoid redoing 5.5a-iii's value-function blowup) — and a bounded, mostly one-time penalty can never outweigh a flat *maximum* reward held for thousands of remaining steps in a ~12,000-step episode. No amount of penalty-side tuning was going to fix that; the flat cap itself was the bug.

Added `HEIGHT_DECAY_RANGE_DEG = 40.0` (`swing_env.py`): height reward now decays linearly from its value at `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` down to 0 over 40 further degrees (80 → 120), floored at 0 (never negative) the rest of the way to the 165 deg limit. Floored rather than going negative on purpose — keeps this a bounded *reduction* of an existing positive term, not a new unbounded penalty. Once at 0, hovering at target keeps earning positive reward every step while pinning at the wall earns nothing further, which removes the "free win" regardless of episode length — something no penalty term can do on its own. Doesn't replace the overshoot-penalty terms, which still supply sharper near-margin gradient; this closes the flat-max gap underneath all of them.

### 5.5a-viii Zero still wasn't enough — the wall is a genuinely stable resting state, needed an active cost

Training resumed under 5.5a-vii's fix. Next round: all 10 eval episodes — deterministic *and* stochastic, action-sampling noise included — converged to 165.4-165.7 deg, a 0.08 deg spread, tighter than anything a deliberately near-target policy had shown so far, and scoring comparably to (occasionally above) the one good near-target episode logged the round before. The fix's direction was right (5.5a-vii's episode did show wall-pinning scoring *below* hovering, the first time that had happened), but the destination was wrong: rather than converging toward the higher-scoring near-target behavior, the policy converged, extremely reliably, onto the wall.

Reading: the hard joint limit (5.8) isn't just a place the policy sometimes ends up — it's a genuinely stable physical resting state. A PD-driven arm pressed against a mechanical stop just sits there, needs essentially no ongoing control effort to stay, and is naturally robust to the disturbance kicks (`DISTURBANCE_PROB_PER_STEP`) that can knock a delicate near-target hover off course. Precisely holding 80 deg against a swinging pendulum's own dynamics is a much harder, higher-variance control problem by comparison. A flat 0 past 120 deg (5.5a-vii) removed the *incentive* to overshoot, but imposed no ongoing *cost* for occupying that easy, robust state once there — not enough pressure to make PPO keep searching for the harder, narrower alternative when the easy one scores nearly as well.

Added `HEIGHT_OVERSHOOT_FLOOR = -0.15` (`swing_env.py`): extended the same linear decay past where it used to clamp at 0, letting it go negative, then floors there. Every step spent pinned at the wall now actively drains episode return instead of merely failing to grow it. Deliberately a small *fixed* constant rather than mirroring the peak height (~1.65) — a penalty that large sustained over a large fraction of a ~12,000-step episode (`self.max_steps`) would reproduce exactly the large-magnitude-outlier-return problem `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP` (5.5a-iii) exists to prevent, just via the height term instead of the overshoot-penalty term, which that cap doesn't cover. -0.15 keeps the worst case (parked at the wall almost the entire episode) bounded around -1,800 — comparable in magnitude to a good episode's positive return (roughly 1,500-2,000), not an order of magnitude beyond it, while still unambiguously flipping pinned time from neutral to costly. Deliberately changed only this, not also `HEIGHT_DECAY_RANGE_DEG`, to get a clean read on whether an ongoing per-step cost — as opposed to a bigger one-time cost — is what's actually needed to make a physically stable attractor unattractive.

### 5.5a-ix Summary stats were misleading — the real problem was a dead startup phase, not the wall

Next round's summary stats looked like a regression toward the earlier wall-lock pattern (spread back down to 7.94 deg, all 10 episodes at 157-166 deg, no episode escaping toward the zero-crossing the way one had the round before). Rather than reflexively tuning `HEIGHT_OVERSHOOT_FLOOR` further, watched the actual policy with `watch_policy.py` first, since summary stats alone can't distinguish "genuinely resting pinned at the wall" from "briefly touching it once or twice during otherwise-normal swinging" — and those need different fixes.

What was actually happening was neither of the two hypotheses that had been driving the last several rounds: only one or two swings per episode ever clearly reached the hard limit, and the swinging itself, once underway, was a clean 80-90 deg oscillation — exactly the target behavior. `peak_angle_deg`'s summary stat can't distinguish "hit 165 once, briefly" from "lived at 165" — a single rare excursion is enough to pull that number to 165 regardless of how good the rest of the episode was, and given how few steps are actually spent past 120 deg, `HEIGHT_OVERSHOOT_FLOOR`'s per-step drain was never going to be a large fraction of episode reward either way. That explains why the last two rounds of reward-shaping moved the needle only modestly: they were correctly reasoned given the aggregate stats available, but aggregate stats were the wrong instrument for what turned out to be a rare, brief event, not a sustained one. Not reverting 5.5a-vi/vii/viii — they're still a correct, harmless, bounded backstop for whenever the wall genuinely is touched — but they were never going to be the main lever here.

The actual biggest problem, only visible by watching: a weird, sustained knee oscillation lasting over a minute — a quarter of a 240s episode — before real swinging even begins. Turned `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` on (0.0 → 0.02, `swing_env.py`) to address it: that constant was deliberately left at 0 since the knee-actuator swap specifically until a working, competent policy existed (see its own comment), precisely to avoid punishing an early policy into never-move-at-all before it discovers pumping. Both trigger conditions in that original comment are now satisfied — a working policy exists, confirmed visually, not just by a summary metric — so this is the intended handoff point, not a new decision. Should discourage the free, unpenalized jitter during that dead startup phase and hopefully shorten or eliminate it, which plausibly recovers far more reward than any further wall-side tuning would have.

### 5.5a-x `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` didn't help — wrong hypothesis, plus evaluate.py's own metric was hiding the real picture

Trained under 5.5a-ix's `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT=0.02` and checked again. Summary stats snapped back to the tightest wall-lock yet (0.05 deg spread, all 10 episodes at 165.4-165.5 deg) — but watching directly told a different story again: startup behavior "pretty much the same, maybe a bit shorter but not meaningfully," and only 3 bumps against the limit total. Two things came out of this:

1. **`ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` was based on the wrong diagnosis.** The startup phase isn't erratic/jittery motion (what that constant penalizes) — it's the knee rotating with a steady, constant pulse around 2/second, "like a toddler swinging its legs on a swing." That's smooth, low-frequency, regular motion; consecutive-step action deltas are small, so `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` barely touches it regardless of its value. A plausible read (not yet confirmed): the swing's natural pendulum frequency for `arm_length=2.0` is roughly 0.35 Hz, and effective parametric pumping generally needs driving at close to twice that (~0.7 Hz) — a 2 Hz knee pulse is well off that, which could explain why it doesn't build amplitude until something else (the reset seed kick, a disturbance kick, or luck) gives the swing's own motion enough definition for the policy to lock onto its actual phase. Not reverting `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` — 0.02 is small and still plausibly helping smooth the good middle section — but not crediting it with something it didn't do either.

2. **The bigger finding: `evaluate.py`'s core metric (`peak_angle_deg`, the single highest angle reached anywhere in an episode) had been actively misleading the whole diagnostic process for at least two rounds running.** A policy that's actually mostly a clean 80-90 deg oscillation with 1-3 brief wall excursions per episode produces the exact same `peak_angle_deg` signature (165ish, near-zero spread) as a policy that's genuinely pinned at the wall for the whole episode — the statistic can't tell those apart, and `auto_train.py`'s entire stopping/promotion gate is built on it. That's a real methodological gap, not just a one-off surprise.

Added two new metrics to `evaluate.py`, reported and logged alongside the existing ones (not replacing them): `time_in_band_frac` (fraction of the whole episode spent within `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` ± `TARGET_BAND_HALF_WIDTH_DEG` = 15 deg) as a majority-behavior statistic a few rare steps can't dominate the way a single max can, and `wall_touch_count` (number of distinct excursions crossing `WALL_TOUCH_THRESHOLD_DEG` = 150 deg from below, not a per-step count) as an explicit rare-event count closer to what "I counted 3 bumps" actually means than any single max-angle number. Deliberately NOT wired into `auto_train.py`'s pass/fail gating yet — there isn't enough data at these new columns to pick sensible thresholds, and guessing thresholds blind would repeat the same mistake this section is about. Let it accumulate in `evaluate_log.csv` across a few more rounds first, then revisit the gate.

### 5.5a-xi Target redefined, 80 → 67.5 deg — margin over height, by explicit request

Not a bug fix like the rest of this saga — a deliberate change in what "good" means, requested directly after watching the policy: even its NORMAL, no-incident cruising height was already running 80-90 deg (past the old 80 target), before counting the rare wall excursions on top of that. Stated preference: regular, robust swinging that maxes out around 65-70 deg and stops pumping there is worth more than a taller policy (even just to the old 80 target) that carries any risk of excursions past it. Part of the reasoning: the 165 deg hard limit that's been the focus of the last five sections is itself a simulation artifact of modeling the rope/chain as a single rigid link (5.7/5.8) — a real flexible chain wouldn't behave predictably anywhere near there, so treating it as a boundary worth approaching at all was arguably the wrong frame. Margin below a real physical failure mode now matters more than height.

Changed, all in `swing_env.py` unless noted:
- `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG`: 80 → 67.5 (midpoint of the stated 65-70 range).
- `HEIGHT_TAPER_START_DEG`: 67.5 → 55.0 — preserves the original 12.5 deg taper width, just anchored under the new target instead of the old one.
- `HEIGHT_DECAY_RANGE_DEG`: 40 → 20 — tightened, not just shifted: the old width left a wide "reward is lower but not really punished" zone out to target+40. 20 reaches the zero/floor crossover at target+20 = 87.5 deg, meaning what used to BE the target (80) now sits solidly in floor territory — a much closer match to "no real tolerance for excursions."
- `OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG`, `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP`, `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`, `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`, `HEIGHT_OVERSHOOT_FLOOR`: left unchanged in value on purpose — all of them key off `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` or the distance to the hard 165 deg limit rather than a hardcoded 80, so they automatically get more aggressive against the same absolute excursion now that target sits lower (e.g. reaching 90 deg used to be a 10 deg overshoot, it's now 22.5). `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`'s worst-case bound moves with it: `0.2 * (165-67.5)**2` = 1,901 (was 1,445).
- `evaluate.py`: docstring's stated target updated; `TARGET_BAND_HALF_WIDTH_DEG` tightened 15 → 10 (at the old width the band's upper edge, 77.5, would still count angles close to the *old* target as "in band" — exactly what's now meant to be avoided). `WALL_TOUCH_THRESHOLD_DEG` (150) left alone — it's about proximity to the physical joint limit, not the reward target, so it doesn't need to track this change.
- `scan_checkpoints.py`: `STREAK_GOOD_MAX_DEG` 95 → 72.5 (same "5 deg slack past the penalty threshold" logic as the original value, recomputed against the new target and current 0 margin). `STREAK_GOOD_MIN_DEG` (55) left alone — its own comment already says it was picked from the collapse/near-collapse range in project history, not from the target.
- `auto_train.py`: **`TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` here is a separate, hand-duplicated constant, not imported from `swing_env.py`** — easy to miss, and missing it would have silently kept gating "reached" on the old 75 deg threshold while the reward now actively punishes anything past ~87.5, blocking the new target from ever registering as reached. Caught and updated: 75 → 62.5 (same "5 deg below the reward cap" logic as before).
- `train.py`: a comment describing the raw per-step height reward's magnitude ("~0 to ~1.65") updated to the new bound (~-0.15 to ~1.23) — descriptive only, not used by any code path, but wrong enough to be worth fixing while everything else here was already getting touched.

Not (yet) touched: `auto_train.py`'s `SPREAD_MAX_DEG`, `ERRATIC_SPREAD_MAX_DEG`, and friends — those are about round-to-round *consistency*, not the target's absolute height, so there's no clear reason they need to move just because the target did. This is a full retarget, not a small nudge — expect the checkpoint that was converging toward ~80-90 deg to need real retraining time to settle around the new, lower, tighter target; resume from the existing checkpoint rather than restarting from scratch (the underlying pumping skill still transfers, only the amplitude target changed), same "resume, don't restart" approach as every other reward-shaping change in this project.

### 5.4a `auto_train.py`'s "log didn't grow" check broke across a mid-run schema reset

First `auto_train.py` run after 5.5a-xi's retarget hit this immediately: round 1 ran `train.py` and `evaluate.py` normally, `evaluate.py` printed its usual "logged this run to evaluate_log.csv" — and then `auto_train.py` reported "evaluate_log.csv didn't grow after that round -- something's wrong with the logging, stopping," even though it very much had.

What actually happened: this was the first time `evaluate.py` had been run since 5.5a-x added its two new columns (`time_in_band_frac`, `wall_touch_count`) to `EXPECTED_HEADER`. `evaluate.py`'s own self-healing logic (the `EXPECTED_HEADER` check, originally added to fix a different bug — see 5.5a-x/earlier) correctly detected the schema mismatch, archived the old (now much shorter of a match) log to a `.bak-<timestamp>` file, and started a brand new `evaluate_log.csv` with just this one row in it. That's working exactly as designed. The break was in `auto_train.py`: it reads `rows_before = len(read_log())` once, before any rounds run in this invocation, then after each round does `new_rows = rows[rows_before:]`. That's a correct way to detect growth under normal appending, but once the file gets reset mid-invocation, `rows` (now just 1 row) is *shorter* than the stale `rows_before` (the old file's much longer history) — slicing a 1-element list from index 50 (say) silently returns an empty list in Python, not an error, so the "no new rows" branch fired even though a real row existed.

This is the same underlying trigger the "Known limitation" note under 5.4 already flagged for `promote_checkpoint.py`'s own archive-and-reset — but that case never actually tripped this specific check before, since a promotion happens as a separate action *between* `auto_train.py` invocations, not silently mid-round inside one the way a schema-migration self-heal can.

Fixed in `auto_train.py`: after reading `rows` each round, if `len(rows) < rows_before`, that can only mean the file was reset since this invocation started (impossible under pure appending) — print a note explaining that, treat every currently-present row as new relative to the stale count, and rebase `rows_before` to 0 so any later rounds in the same invocation keep slicing correctly against the new, smaller file. No data was actually lost either time (the old log is still sitting in its `.bak-*` file, same as always) — this was purely a false "stopped, something's wrong" report from a correct reset being read by a check that assumed the file could only ever grow.

### 5.5a-xii Repeated wall-bouncing wasn't discouraged at all after the first touch — added a per-event penalty

Post-retarget training was volatile round to round (peak angle swinging 160 → 37 → 10.5 → 103 → 90 across five rounds), including one stretch that looked like a real skill regression (deterministic reward near-zero, 3/5 episodes barely reaching 10 deg) before recovering on its own the very next round once given more training time — a case where waiting, not intervening, was the right call, since the reward gradient already favored real pumping (the stochastic side kept finding strongly positive reward at reasonable heights throughout, evidence the underlying capability was never actually lost).

What *didn't* self-correct across six rounds: spread stayed wide (140 → 14 → 93 → 11 → 132 → 139 deg) with no narrowing trend, and — visible now specifically because of 5.5a-x's `wall_touch_count` metric — individual DETERMINISTIC episodes (the mode that matters for real deployment) logged 28 and 8 distinct wall touches in a single episode. Not one bad excursion pulling `peak_angle_deg` up; a sustained pattern of repeatedly bouncing off the physical limit throughout the episode. The opposite of "regular, robust swinging" (5.5a-xi's explicit ask), and unlike the near-zero-angle dip above, there was no structural reason to expect more training to fix this on its own: neither existing overshoot mechanism actually discourages repetition. `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP`'s budget exhausts within the first ~15-20 steps of any real overshoot and stays exhausted regardless of how many more times the wall gets touched afterward; `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` only charges on the step that sets a NEW worst-ever `overshoot_deg` for the episode, so a second, third, or twenty-eighth touch to a similar or lower height than the first costs nothing under either mechanism. A structural incentive gap, not something more training time closes by itself — the same kind of thing 5.5a-vi through viii each found and fixed with a new, narrowly-scoped mechanism, not by waiting.

Added `WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY = 20.0` (`swing_env.py`, alongside `WALL_TOUCH_THRESHOLD_DEG = 150.0`, matching `evaluate.py`'s diagnostic constant of the same name/value but kept as its own separate constant for the same reason as `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG`'s duplication into `auto_train.py`, 5.5a-xi): tracks `self.past_wall_threshold` (reset in `reset()`) and charges a small fixed cost once per distinct crossing from below the threshold to above it — mirrors exactly how `evaluate.py` already computes `wall_touch_count`, just now feeding the actual reward instead of only being reported. Fixed and per-event on purpose, not scaled by severity or duration (that's already the other two mechanisms' job): 1 touch costs a mild 20, comparable to an isolated, quickly-corrected excursion; 28 touches costs 560, clearly and substantially bad. Bounded without an explicit cap: the swing's own physical oscillation period (roughly 2.8s per cycle near resonance, 5.5a-x) limits how many times it could plausibly re-touch the limit within one ~240s episode even in a worst case (~85 touches, ~1,700 total) — the same rough order of magnitude as `HEIGHT_OVERSHOOT_FLOOR`'s bound, not a new blowup risk.

### 5.5a-xiii Too much punishment relative to reward — the policy retreated to barely moving, restored a small margin

`WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY` worked exactly as intended (confirmed: wall touches dropped to 0 across all 10 episodes in the very next round) — but that same round's `auto_train.py` check flagged a cliff (deterministic peak 89.8 → 21.1 deg). Read as expected transitional noise at first, same pattern as an earlier scare that had self-corrected within one round once given more training time. It didn't recover this time: the round after that declined further (14.6 deg), and — the deciding factor — the STOCHASTIC side weakened too (only 1 of 5 episodes finding real height, down from 3 of 5 the round before). `auto_train.py`'s decline check fired on 4 straight falling rounds (103 → 90 → 21 → 15 deg, an 88.6 deg total drop). Unlike the earlier dip, the evidence the underlying capability was intact (broad stochastic success) was itself eroding — the trigger for actually intervening rather than waiting again.

Wall touches were already 0 in both declining rounds, so `WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY` wasn't the thing directly being tripped — something upstream of it plausibly made even *attempting* a real pump look too risky. Prime suspect: with `OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG` at 0, the quadratic `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` penalty starts the instant a step crosses `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG`, and since target moved down 12.5 deg in 5.5a-xi (80 → 67.5), that penalty is now considerably harsher at any given *absolute* angle than before — e.g. at 85 deg, `overshoot_deg` is now 17.5 instead of 5, a roughly 12x larger quadratic penalty for physically the same amount of overshoot. A policy still readapting through two stacked landscape changes in a row (the retarget, then the wall-touch penalty) may have rationally learned that a real pump attempt risks a large, immediate penalty on any imprecise landing — making "barely move" look safer, even though it scores far worse when a pump actually lands (982.8 reward for one successful stochastic episode that round, vs. roughly 15-65 for the barely-moving majority).

Restored `OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG`, 0.0 → 5.0 (`swing_env.py`) — partial, not full: the original pre-5.5a-iv value was 10.0. Safer to bring back some margin now than it was to remove it originally, because the mechanisms added *after* that removal — `HEIGHT_TAPER_START_DEG` easing reward growth before target, `HEIGHT_DECAY_RANGE_DEG` reducing reward immediately past target — already supply continuous corrective pressure on their own, regardless of this margin; restoring it only delays the *additional* quadratic penalty by a few degrees, giving a still-adapting policy some room to land near target without an immediate large penalty for modest imprecision. Deliberately left `HEIGHT_DECAY_RANGE_DEG` and `WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY` untouched to get a clean read on whether this alone resolves the retreat.

### 5.5a-xv Wall-pinning recurred despite 5.5a-xii's penalty — raised it 20 → 200

Ran a fresh `scan_checkpoints.py` pass (resumed from an Aug 5 snapshot through step 47.66M, chunked forward to the current step ~50.7M) as a general health check, not in response to any specific alarm. Bounded against reproducing 5.5a-iii's value-function blowup: a single touch now costs 200 (~10-13% of a typical good episode's 1,500-2,000 return, on its own — no longer "barely different from an isolated excursion" the way 20 was); the 6-touch count seen in the most recent stochastic round costs 1,200 (over double `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP`'s entire budget, appropriately dominant since repetition is exactly what this term targets); the highest touch count ever observed in this project (28, from 5.5a-xii's original motivating data) costs 5,600 — large and clearly punishing, but still nowhere near the five-to-six-figure range (1,269,687 value loss off one -65,289 return) that `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP` exists to prevent reproducing. Deliberately left `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` and `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` untouched this round, same single-variable discipline as 5.5a-xiii's margin restoration, to get a clean read on whether this term alone is enough this time.

### 5.5a-xvi Wall touches were only ever discouraged, never prevented — episode now ends on first contact

5.5a-xv raised `WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY` 20 → 200 but changed nothing structural about what happens once a run reaches the wall: `terminated` was hardcoded `False` (`swing_env.py`'s `step()`, "nothing in this task counts as failure yet") and the only way an episode ever ends is `truncated` on the ~12,000-step time limit. The very next round after 5.5a-xv's change confirmed the gap directly: one deterministic episode logged 11 distinct wall touches, 11 × 200 = 2,200 in penalty alone, matching a real -2,239.9 episode return — the larger penalty was working exactly as designed (each touch is now expensive) but nothing stopped the same episode from touching it 11 separate times, because nothing ends the episode after the first.

No penalty magnitude closes this on its own — a bigger number only raises the cost of repetition, it doesn't remove the option. Structural fix instead: `terminated = past_wall_now` (`swing_env.py`, alongside the existing `WALL_TOUCH_THRESHOLD_DEG`/`WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY` logic, no new constant needed). The episode now ends on the first crossing into wall territory, full stop — repeated in-episode wall contact is now structurally impossible rather than merely costly, and the wall's "stable resting state" property (5.5a-viii's finding: a rod wedged against a mechanical stop needs no ongoing control effort, unlike a precise near-target hover) is removed outright, since there's no more episode left to rest through. Verified directly: forcing `qpos` to just past `WALL_TOUCH_THRESHOLD_DEG` and stepping once produces `terminated=True` on that exact step.

Deliberately scoped to the wall specifically, not the graduated overshoot terms below it (`OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`, `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`) — an excursion to, say, 90 deg is still a "keep training, get better at this" problem; only actually reaching the 150 deg wall gets the hard line. Downstream check: no other file (`train.py`, `evaluate.py`, `watch_policy.py`) reads or branches on `terminated` directly — SB3/gymnasium's `done = terminated or truncated` handling is generic, so this needed no changes anywhere else. One expected side effect worth watching for in the next few rounds: episodes that do reach the wall will now be much shorter than the full episode length, which changes the episode-length distribution PPO trains against — not expected to cause the value-function blowup 5.5a-iii guarded against (a short, sharply-terminated episode is the opposite of the unbounded-length outlier-return problem that caused that), but worth confirming against TensorBoard the way 5.5a-xiv did if anything looks off.

### 5.5a-xvii First-touch termination was cutting off successful climbs, not just failures — softened to second touch

5.5a-xvi's fix (end the episode on the first wall crossing) cleanly solved what it targeted: repeated in-episode bouncing dropped from 11 touches in a single episode to 1. But a fresh `scan_checkpoints.py`-style trend check across 8 checkpoints spread from step 40M to the current 64.9M found the deterministic phase-lock catch rate (fraction of episodes reaching real height) had gone from a steady 50-75% (40M-54.2M) to 0% across three separate checkpoints spanning 57.8M-64.9M — a real, sustained collapse, not noise (0 of 4 three times running, after consistently getting some strong episodes every round before).

Investigated the mechanism directly rather than assumed it. Action magnitude in the failing checkpoint's episodes is NOT reduced — knee action amplitude runs 0.93-1.0 there, same or higher than the working checkpoint's 0.92-0.95 — ruling out "learned to hold back to avoid the wall" as the story, since a genuinely risk-averse policy would show less effort, not equal-or-greater effort producing far less height. What differs is timing: the working checkpoint's successful episodes show a tight, locked rhythm (knee action period ~64 steps, angle response ~85 steps, closely matched); the failing checkpoint's episodes are full-effort but never establish that lock.

Checked `tb_logs/PPO_1`'s `rollout/ep_len_mean` (actual training rollout length, not eval) for direct evidence of what changed: flat at 12,000 (no early terminations at all) for nearly the entire project history before 5.5a-xvi, as expected since `terminated` was hardcoded `False` before that. A real, measurable dip (10,920-11,980) appears in exactly the 55.7M-62.3M window right after 5.5a-xvi took effect — confirming a genuine burst of early-terminated training episodes coincided with the catch-rate collapse. But that dip had already subsided back to ~12,000 by 64.4M onward, meaning this doesn't look like an *ongoing* data-starvation effect happening right now so much as a turbulent transition window the policy hasn't recovered from since.

The more direct finding: at the working 54.2M checkpoint, of episodes that reached real height (peak ≥50 deg), HALF ended by riding straight into the wall — action amplitude was statistically indistinguishable between the ones that stopped short (~100-115 deg) and the ones that didn't (150+ deg). At this stage of skill, "commit to a real pump" and "risk touching the wall" were substantially the same strategy, not separable ones. 5.5a-xvi's first-touch termination was landing on a large fraction of genuinely successful climbs, not just the repeated-bouncing failures it was built to stop.

Changed `swing_env.py`: added `WALL_TERMINATE_AFTER_TOUCHES = 2` and a `self.wall_touch_count_this_episode` counter (reset in `reset()`). The first crossing into `WALL_TOUCH_THRESHOLD_DEG` still charges `WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY` (200) as before, but no longer ends the episode — a trajectory that briefly overshoots as part of an otherwise-good climb gets the chance to settle back down and keep running, keeping the full-length experience in the training data instead of being cut off instantly. A SECOND distinct crossing — the actual signature of repeated bouncing, which is what originally motivated 5.5a-xii/xvi — still ends the episode immediately, so the original 11/28-touch pattern is still cut short almost as fast as before. Verified directly: forcing the joint above threshold once no longer terminates (crossing count 1), dropping back below and forcing it above a second time does (crossing count 2).

2 is a reasoned starting point, not tuned, and whether it actually restores the catch rate is an open question this change alone doesn't answer — it needs real training time under it, not just a smoke test, to find out.

### 5.5a-xviii Entropy has been quietly collapsing this whole project — added an explicit ent_coef

Two rounds after 5.5a-xvii's softened wall termination, deterministic peak angle stayed low (16.9 deg) with a notably TIGHT spread (14.77 deg, versus the 34-99 deg spreads recent rounds had been showing) — a real narrowing, not the usual noisy bounce, and worth a closer look given the concern raised directly: low peaks are fine, but this looked stuck low rather than settling near target.

Checked `tb_logs/PPO_1`'s `train/entropy_loss` (SB3 logs the negative of policy entropy under this name, so a RISING value means falling entropy) across its full history: roughly stable in the 0.9-1.8 range for most of training, through step ~61.1M. No sign of leveling off across that ~17M-step stretch. Traced the cause: `train.py`'s `PPO(...)` call has never set `ent_coef` -- SB3 defaults it to 0.0, meaning there has been zero explicit pressure keeping exploration alive for this entire project. The recent narrowing-spread pattern is consistent with what an unopposed entropy collapse looks like: the action distribution getting too confident/narrow to keep exploring toward the harder-to-discover, more rewarding real-height strategy, converging instead on a low-effort, easily-repeatable ~15-20 deg oscillation it can produce with high confidence -- not convergence toward a good answer, convergence toward a safe one.

Added `ENT_COEF = 0.01` (`train.py`) and passed it explicitly on BOTH the `PPO.load()` resume path and the fresh-init `PPO(...)` path -- deliberately not repeating the exact bug class 5.5a-xi's gamma fix hit, where an override existed as a constant but wasn't actually passed into `PPO.load()`, so it silently never took effect on any resumed run (which is almost every run, since checkpoint files exist after the first). 0.01 is a common starting value for continuous-action PPO's entropy bonus, reasoned not tuned to this environment specifically. Watch `train/entropy_loss` over the next several rounds -- if it stops climbing (or starts falling), that's the entropy bonus doing its job; if the deterministic spread starts finding real height again rather than just staying narrow at a low value, that's the actual goal.

If it resumes the same climb, the problem is more likely the mechanism itself (a flat entropy bonus alone isn't enough to durably oppose whatever is driving the collapse) than the magnitude, and a different approach would be worth considering rather than a fourth escalation.

### 5.5a-xx 0.03 overshot into saturated-noise territory — dialed back to 0.015, and started checking action std directly

5.5a-xix's `ent_coef=0.03` looked, at first, like a repeat of the same collapse it was meant to fix -- `entropy_loss` kept falling (not climbing back like at 0.01) and BOTH `evaluate.py` sides stayed collapsed to a tight, low cluster (2.14 deg spread). But this time checked the policy's actual action std directly (`model.policy.log_std.exp()`, the same value `evaluate.py` prints at startup) instead of only reading `train/entropy_loss`, and found the real problem: `[2.21, 2.79]`. Action range is `[-1, 1]` (width 2) -- a Gaussian with std over 2 puts most of its mass outside that range, so sampled actions were landing on `step()`'s hard clip almost regardless of the mean, close to a coin flip between the two extremes each step. That's not exploration around a promising strategy, it's noise incapable of producing the smooth, phase-locked knee/lean rhythm a real pump needs -- which is why entropy kept climbing (in the "more exploration" direction) while performance didn't improve at all, and why `clip_fraction`/`approx_kl` both dropped too (2.3-6%, 0.003-0.006) -- with actions this saturated there's little clean advantage signal left for the policy gradient to learn from, so the mean barely updates either. No PPO-health crisis underneath it (`explained_variance`, `value_loss` both fine) -- purely an entropy-bonus overshoot into a degenerate, saturated-noise regime.

Lowered `ENT_COEF` 0.03 -> 0.015 in `train.py` -- a much smaller step off the original 0.01 this time (not another 3x jump), given how much more sensitive the 0.01->0.03 jump turned out to be than the `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`/`WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY` precedent that motivated it (went from clearly too weak to clearly counterproductive with no obviously good middle ground yet found). Same verification as before: checkpoint currently has `0.03` baked in, override correctly reports `0.015`.

Changed what to watch for next time: `model.policy.log_std.exp()` directly, not just `train/entropy_loss`. Entropy_loss alone can't distinguish "healthy increased exploration" from "saturated noise" -- both move it in the same direction. Something in the rough 0.3-0.8 std range (meaningful per-step variation without saturating the `[-1, 1]` clip) is the actual target.

### 5.5a-xxi 0.015 didn't reverse the saturation either — direct log_std reset instead of a fourth ent_coef tweak

A round after 5.5a-xx's `ent_coef` cut to 0.015, `model.policy.log_std.exp()` had gotten WORSE, not better: `[2.94, 4.15]`, up from `[2.21, 2.79]` under the higher 0.03 setting. `train/entropy_loss` was still falling further negative too (last 6 values -5.69 -> -5.76), not leveling off. Checked again before proposing anything further and it had kept climbing since: `[3.65, 5.15]`.

Two tweaks in a row moving the wrong direction ruled out "just needs a smaller nudge." Checked whether leftover Adam optimizer momentum from the 0.03 era was the culprit, since `PPO.load()` restores `policy.optimizer`'s state dict (including per-parameter `exp_avg`/`exp_avg_sq`) same as it does the weights, on every resume — not just theorized, inspected directly: Adam step count was 7,448,759 with default betas (0.9, 0.999), giving the 2nd moment an effective memory of roughly the last ~1,000 steps, negligible against the total. `exp_avg` was small and mixed-sign (`[0.014, -0.033]`), so the live gradient signal wasn't uniformly pushing log_std up anymore — ruled out lingering momentum as the driver.

The actual mechanism: the log_std *weight* itself is what every `PPO.load()` resume carries forward unconditionally, and once std is this far past the `[-1,1]` action range, sampled actions land on `step()`'s hard clip almost regardless of the mean — same saturated-noise dynamic 5.5a-xx diagnosed, but now confirmed durable, not transient. That destroys the clean advantage signal a policy gradient needs to correct it, while Gaussian entropy is unbounded (grows without limit as std grows) so any positive `ent_coef` still supplies some upward pressure on top. Nothing in ordinary training was going to unwind this on a reasonable timescale — the weight needed direct correction, not a fourth coefficient guess.

Wrote `reset_log_std.py`: backs up `swing_policy.zip`, loads the model, overwrites `model.policy.log_std.data` to a fixed `log(0.3)` (std = 0.3, chosen at the conservative end of the 0.3-0.8 target range from 5.5a-xx given how badly this overshot last time), clears the Adam optimizer state for that one parameter specifically (its `exp_avg`/`exp_avg_sq` were calibrated for gradients around a totally different, saturated operating point — leaving them in place risked a distorted first update at the new one), and saves back to `swing_policy.zip`. No other policy/value weights, VecNormalize stats, or hyperparameters touched. `ENT_COEF` left at 0.015 (unchanged) since the coefficient itself was never confirmed to be the actual problem.

This is a different class of change than anything else in this log — every prior fix changed reward/termination/hyperparameter *code* going forward; this one directly edits already-trained weights. Verified by reloading the saved checkpoint fresh from disk after writing it: `log_std = [-1.204, -1.204]` -> `std = [0.300, 0.300]`, optimizer state for that parameter empty as expected. Backup of the pre-reset checkpoint kept at `swing_policy_pre_logstd_reset_backup.zip` in case this needs to be undone. What to watch next: whether std climbs back toward saturation again under the same `ent_coef=0.015` (would mean 0.015 is still too high even as a resting value, not just as a correction rate) or holds in a healthy range while `evaluate.py` performance recovers.

### 5.5a-xxii ent_coef alone wasn't holding std down even from a reset — added a hard structural cap, lowered ent_coef again

Exactly the thing 5.5a-xxi flagged to watch for happened, fast. One round after the reset (std=0.30), `log_std.exp()` had grown only modestly, to `[0.49, 0.42]` — still healthy, and `train/approx_kl`/`clip_fraction` had recovered nicely (up to 0.0135/15%, from the saturated regime's 0.003-0.004/2-3%), with `rollout/ep_rew_mean` climbing 45.4 -> 58.3. But the round immediately after that, std roughly doubled again to `[1.08, 0.93]` — past the 0.3-0.8 target range, with a Gaussian at std=1.0 already landing close to a third of its mass outside the `[-1,1]` action range. TensorBoard for that window showed the same signature reversing: `entropy_loss` resumed climbing (-1.26 -> -2.81), `approx_kl`/`clip_fraction` fell back down again (to 0.006-0.008/6-8%), and `ep_rew_mean` growth stalled flat.

This is a materially stronger finding than 5.5a-xx/xxi's original overshoot: TWO independent starting points — the original organically-saturated checkpoint, and a freshly reset-to-0.30 checkpoint — both climbed back toward saturation within a handful of million steps under the exact same `ent_coef=0.015`. That rules out "this specific checkpoint's state was unlucky" and points at the coefficient itself not being a stable resting value in this environment: Gaussian entropy is unbounded (grows without limit as std grows), so any positive `ent_coef` supplies persistent upward pressure, and once std is large enough that actions start saturating the `[-1,1]` clip, the resulting weak/noisy advantage signal isn't reliably enough to push back — there's no natural brake, just a race between the entropy bonus and however strong the reward-driven counter-pressure happens to be at the time (and it apparently isn't strong enough yet, since real height still isn't being found).

Rather than a fourth attempt at guessing the right ent_coef magnitude, added a structural fix instead: `LogStdClipCallback` (`train.py`), a new `BaseCallback` that clamps `model.policy.log_std` in place to `[log(LOG_STD_MIN_STD), log(LOG_STD_MAX_STD)]` = std `[0.15, 0.6]`, once per rollout via `_on_rollout_start` — timed to run right after each gradient update completes and before the next batch of experience is collected, so it always acts on the freshly-updated parameters. This bounds both failure modes this project has now hit directly and unconditionally: can't fully collapse toward near-deterministic (floor at 0.15, well above zero) the way pre-`ent_coef` training did (5.5a-xviii), and can't saturate the action clip (ceiling at 0.6) the way `ent_coef=0.03` and then `0.015` both eventually did — regardless of what `ent_coef` or the reward landscape are doing. `_on_rollout_start` also fires once before the very first rollout of any `learn()` call, including a resumed run, so simply adding this callback and resuming training immediately clamps the currently out-of-range `[1.08, 0.93]` checkpoint back into range — no separate manual reset script needed this time (5.5a-xxi's `reset_log_std.py` was a one-time fix for a checkpoint that predated this callback).

With the cap now doing the structural job, also lowered `ENT_COEF` 0.015 -> 0.005 (`train.py`) — a more conservative value than would have felt safe to try before, now that it can no longer single-handedly cause a full collapse or explosion; its role shifts from "the only thing preventing both failure modes" to "fine-tuning where std settles within an already-safe range."

Verified directly before shipping: loaded the current (out-of-range) `swing_policy.zip`, confirmed `log_std.exp()` reads `[1.08, 0.93]`, then called the callback's `_on_rollout_start()` against it directly (standalone, not via a full `learn()` call) and confirmed it clamps to exactly `[0.60, 0.60]`. Separately forced `log_std` down to an artificially tiny std=0.01 and reran the same clamp, confirmed it floors back up to `0.15`. Both ends verified working before relying on it. The on-disk checkpoint itself was left untouched by this test (loaded fresh, modified only in memory, never re-saved) — the actual correction happens for real the next time `train.py` runs and resumes from it.

What to watch next: `model.policy.log_std.exp()` should now never read outside `[0.15, 0.6]` regardless of round outcome — if it ever does, that means this callback broke or got bypassed somehow, which would itself be worth a hard stop. Within that range, watch where it settles under the lower `ent_coef=0.005`, and whether `evaluate.py` performance actually starts recovering now that a full round can complete without re-saturating partway through.

### 5.9f Overshoot penalty redesigned to reset per-excursion, not per-episode -- the real answer to "what's the incentive to overshoot"

Prompted by the user directly asking why the policy would keep overshooting when a penalty exists for it. Worked through the reward math by hand first: height reward actively decays past target (not just flat) and the overshoot penalty is quadratic, so on paper overshooting should look bad. Then tested the user's own intuition that simply holding the actuators still near target ("coast") should avoid overshoot -- built a version of `manual_pump.py`'s reference law that freezes lean/knee targets at a threshold and lets momentum carry it the rest of the way. Confirmed directly: coasting from 55/60/63 deg only carries another ~0.5-1 deg from momentum, nothing like the trained policy's 10+ deg excursions -- the user's intuition was right, and the earlier claim in this conversation that "precise throttling is a hard control problem" was wrong and is retracted. Coasting is mechanically easy; the real issue was on the reward side.

Measured that side directly against `best_model`: the UNCAPPED overshoot penalty for a real episode would total 21,553 across 118 separate excursions (2,187 steps / 43.7s spent past the margin) -- but `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP`'s budget (500, under `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT=1.2`) exhausts in about 4 steps (0.08s) at a typical ~10 deg overshoot excess. Since that budget only ever reset once per EPISODE (in `reset()`), not once per excursion, it was only ever taxing the FIRST excursion each episode -- the other 117 that episode were entirely free. That's the direct, concrete answer to "what's the incentive": repeating the overshoot costs nothing once the first excursion has spent the shared budget, so a policy that's going to overshoot at all loses nothing by doing it on every cycle instead of once.

Fixed by making `self.cumulative_overshoot_penalty` (`swing_env.py`) reset at the start of every fresh excursion (the step `overshoot_deg` first goes positive), mirroring `WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY`'s existing crossing-detection pattern exactly, instead of only in `reset()`. Considered simply raising the per-episode cap instead, but rejected it: no fixed per-episode number can make EVERY cycle cost something for a policy that overshoots repeatedly, short of setting it so high it stops bounding anything -- the per-excursion reset is what the user's stated goal ("overshoot costs every cycle") actually requires architecturally, not just a bigger version of the same mechanism.

Also lowered the cap itself, 500 -> 100, since reapplying the OLD 500-per-episode value on every excursion (with ~118-127 excursions/episode typical for a chronic overshooter) would let the worst-case episode total climb to 50,000-60,000 -- squarely back in the range that caused the original crisis this mechanism exists to prevent (`train/value_loss` spiking to 1,269,687 on a -65,289 episode return, documented in this same constant's history). 100 per excursion keeps the realistic worst-case total (100-127 excursions x 100 = 10,000-12,700) well clear of that danger zone while still being far more punishing IN TOTAL than the old design's effective ~500 ceiling, since that total is now actually reachable through genuine repeated overshoot rather than sitting mostly unused after the first excursion. Also preserves most of the quadratic severity gradient through a typical excursion (reaches cap around overshoot_deg ~9.1 deg, close to the ~10-13 deg peaks actually observed, not before them).

Verified directly against `best_model`: total episode overshoot penalty under the new design is 11,489.7 (safely inside the reasoned range), with 124 of 127 excursions (97.6%) now carrying a meaningful (>1.0) penalty instead of just the first -- individual excursion penalties ramp correctly with severity (0.2, 0.4, 44.8, 62.5, ... up to the 100.0 cap) rather than being flat. 30-step random-action smoke test runs clean. Reward-coefficient/mechanism change only, same class as 5.9e -- no archive/fresh-start needed, training continues from the current lineage. ### 5.9h Deterministic bootstrapping failure isolated and tracked: not the knee, not the seed-kick size -- the mean-action policy's own capture basin is fragile and untracked

### 5.9k `train.py` now resumes from `best_model` instead of `swing_policy` when they've diverged -- root-caused the failure mode first

The very next round after `N_EVAL_EPISODES` was raised (5.9j) exercised the new dual-checkpoint system for real: `swing_policy`'s report looked fine on the surface but its `best_model` counterpart that same round came back with a real problem, and over the following two rounds `swing_policy` kept degrading -- clean (72.4 deg/3.3 deg spread) -> spread blown out (65.7/66.0) -> mean dropping too, with actual bootstrap failures in BOTH deterministic (4/15) and stochastic (5/15) modes for the first time. Root-caused before changing anything: `rollout/ep_rew_mean` (the stochastic training signal) and every PPO health metric (`value_loss`, `std`, `explained_variance`, `approx_kl`, `clip_fraction`) looked completely healthy across all three rounds -- ruled out the historical crisis pattern immediately. A policy can be optimizing completely normally in aggregate while its capture basin for this one behavior quietly narrows or shifts from one ordinary update to the next, with zero warning in anything PPO reports about itself.

Given that, the actionable fix isn't a training-stability change (there was no instability to find) -- it's not compounding on top of a state that turned out worse than one already in hand. Added `pick_resume_source()` to `train.py`: at resume time, compares `evaluate_log.csv`'s most recent `best_model` and `swing_policy` rows (both logged every round since 5.9i); if `swing_policy` shows any bootstrap failures (det or sto) while `best_model` shows none, resumes training from `best_model` instead. Falls back to the historical default (resume from `swing_policy`) if the log doesn't have both rows yet, predates the bootstrap-fail-count columns, or the chosen files don't actually exist on disk -- same fail-open discipline as `auto_train.py`'s row-pairing logic (5.9i). This only changes what gets loaded FROM at the start of a run; `MODEL_PATH`/`VECNORM_PATH` (`swing_policy`/`vec_normalize.pkl`) remain unchanged as where every run SAVES to at the end, so the overall step count and lineage continue exactly as before.

Verified directly: ran `pick_resume_source()` against the real `evaluate_log.csv` (which has the round described above) and confirmed it correctly selects `best_model` with the right reasoning. Also tested against synthetic logs covering every fallback path -- no log file, both checkpoints clean, `swing_policy` bad but `best_model` files missing from disk, pre-5.9h schema with no bootstrap columns, and the real "should switch" case with the files actually present -- all five behaved exactly as designed.

Not solved by this: the underlying fragility itself (an ordinary gradient step can still narrow the capture basin from a `best_model` starting point too) -- this is a mitigation against compounding on a known-bad state, not a fix for the fragility existing in the first place. Also updated `train.py`'s module docstring, which still referenced the old "edit `MODEL_PATH`/`VECNORM_PATH` at the top of `evaluate.py`" workflow superseded by 5.9i.

### 5.9l `TARGET_OVERSHOOT_MAX` raised 0.1 -> 75.0, root-caused via disturbance ablation rather than just loosened

A new batch of rounds after 5.9k came back completely clean on every metric this project already tracks -- 0 bootstrap failures on both checkpoints across 6 straight rounds, angle consistently 70-73 deg, spread under 7 deg every time -- but the "target reached" streak kept resetting to 0 anyway. `TARGET_OVERSHOOT_MAX = 0.1` was the only thing failing: 5 of the 6 rounds' combined (worse-of-both-checkpoints) overshoot penalty landed between 15 and 60, not near-0.1's implicit "should be exactly 0.00" bar.

Investigated before touching the number, since `BOOTSTRAP_FAIL_MAX_ANGLE_DEG` (5.9h) was set from a real bimodal gap and this deserved the same discipline, not a reflexive loosening. First checked whether overshoot penalty was actually noise the way N=5 sampling was (5.9j) -- it isn't: `swing_env.py`'s `overshoot_penalty` is exactly 0 for any episode whose peak angle never crosses `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG + OVERSHOOT_MARGIN_DEG` (67.5 + 5 = 72.5 deg), and `evaluate.py` logs the MEAN of the 15 episodes' own peaks, not the round's max -- so a round averaging 70-73 deg is fully consistent with several of its 15 episodes individually crossing 72.5 deg, a real (if usually small) excursion, not a sampling artifact.

Ran a direct ablation on `best_model` to find out why episodes were crossing that line at all: 8 deterministic episodes with `DISTURBANCE_PROB_PER_STEP` forced to 0 vs. its real 0.001/step default. Disturbances OFF: all 8 episodes converged to a tight 69.3-69.4 deg, exactly 0.0 overshoot penalty every single time -- proof the reward shape (`OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`, `HEIGHT_DECAY_RANGE_DEG`, `PEAK_OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT`, `WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY`) is doing its job correctly and needs no retuning. Disturbances ON (the real setting): peaks 71.4-73.3 deg, overshoot 0.0-3.6/episode. The residual overshoot is the environment's own RNG -- `DISTURBANCE_PROB_PER_STEP`'s random mid-episode gust kicks (deliberately turned on for robustness training, section 7 roadmap item 1) landing while the swing happens to already be near peak amplitude -- not imprecise control. Cross-checked wall-touch counts on even the worst "healthy" overshoot rounds logged historically (392, 398, 618) -- all 0 -- confirming these are small, bounded, recovered-from excursions, not the wall-pinning runaway pattern this project has hit before (5.5a-vii, 5.5a-xv).

Conclusion: with disturbance training on (an intentional, permanent part of the task, not something to disable), `TARGET_OVERSHOOT_MAX` near 0 is close to structurally unreachable regardless of policy quality -- it was gating on the environment's RNG, not on the policy. Unlike the bootstrap-failure metric, overshoot penalty has no clean bimodal gap to calibrate against here -- it's continuous disturbance noise, so the new value is a reasoned judgment call, not a measured boundary: recent combined readings on genuinely healthy rounds (good angle, tight spread, zero bootstrap fails) topped out at 59.75 (only 6 rounds of evidence in this exact regime). Raised to 75.0 -- real margin above that observed max, while staying far below `ERRATIC_OVERSHOOT_MAX=400` (the separate "something is badly wrong" check, unchanged) and `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP`'s per-excursion ceiling of 100.

Verified directly: ran the updated `read_log()`/`streak_from_history()` against the real `evaluate_log.csv` -- all of the last 6 rounds now pass, giving a streak of 6/3 already seeded from history. **Practical consequence:** the very next `auto_train.py` invocation will very likely declare "target reached" after just one more passing round, since the streak requirement is already satisfied by existing history. Worth reviewing with `mjpython watch_policy.py` when that happens rather than assuming more rounds are still needed.

### 5.9m `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` raised 0.02 -> 1.0 (branch experiment) -- the trained policy moves 2-4x faster than a smooth reference, not just visually "spastic"

After watching `watch_policy.py` on the now fully-converged policy (5.9l), the user flagged the motion itself as a concern separate from swing quality: "spastic", not human-plausible, especially at the knee -- worth investigating even though the policy is otherwise working well, and explicitly NOT worth losing the current working lineage over.

Quantified before touching anything. Oscillation FREQUENCY was nearly identical (~1.8 Hz trained vs ~1.9-2.3 Hz reference, via zero-crossing counts) -- the policy isn't thrashing at a higher frequency than a smooth human-style pump. But mean angular velocity was 2-4x higher, concentrated mostly in the knee: lean 103 deg/s vs 55 deg/s reference; knee 206 deg/s vs 51 deg/s reference (max knee velocity 607 vs 223 deg/s). Same rhythm, much sharper snaps within each cycle -- confirms the visual read was real, not just an impression.

Mean 0.028, p90 0.079, p99 0.31, max 1.20 (theoretical max for a full both-axes -1->+1 flip is 8, so even the worst real step falls well short of that). At the old weight (0.02), even the single worst observed step only cost 0.024 in reward -- negligible next to a per-step height reward near target of ~1.5-1.6. At 1.0: mean-case cost stays small (~0.028, under 2% of a good step's height reward), which should avoid reproducing the "give up and stop moving" collapse `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT`'s own original staged-rollout reasoning warns about (5.5-era, why this was 0.0 until a working policy existed) -- but the rare large snaps (p99 and up) now cost up to ~1.2, comparable to an entire good step's height reward, real targeted pressure on the specific tail behavior that looked spastic, not a uniform tax on all movement.

Set up as a genuine branch, not an overwrite, per the user's explicit request not to risk the current working lineage: copied `swing_policy.zip`, `vec_normalize.pkl`, `best_model/best_model.zip`, `best_model/best_vec_normalize.pkl`, and a snapshot of `evaluate_log.csv` at the branch point into `pre_action_rate_experiment_backup_<timestamp>/`, matching this project's existing `pre_promotion_backup_*`/`pre_rollback_backup_*` convention. Training resumes completely normally from the live files as always (nothing about `train.py`'s resume logic changed) -- the backup exists purely so the current fast-but-effective lineage can be restored if this experiment costs too much swing height. `checkpoints/` itself needed no backup -- checkpoint files are step-numbered and never overwritten, so the full step history stays intact regardless of which branch training continues on.

Verified: `swing_env.py` still imports and smoke-tests cleanly (fresh env reset + step) with the new weight active.

Not yet run: this only changes what future training rounds optimize against -- no rounds have been trained under the new weight yet. Watch for after resuming: (a) motion visibly smoother in `watch_policy.py` with peak angle/reliability holding up -- success, keep this branch; (b) angle/reliability dropping substantially -- 1.0 may be too strong, back off, or restore the backup and reconsider; (c) motion still looking spastic with no angle cost -- 1.0 wasn't enough, go bigger (same "still not enough" signature `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` hit twice before landing on 1.2).

### 5.9t Can `swing_angle` be recovered from an onboard IMU instead of a direct sensor? Tested in simulation, not just reasoned about

A rotary encoder at the pivot (roadmap section 7's old "obvious direct sensor" suggestion) isn't practical for the real build, per the user. The fallback question -- can an accelerometer (gyro included, i.e. a basic 6-DOF IMU) recover swing_angle well enough -- was tested directly in simulation rather than answered from first principles.

WHY THE SEAT, NOT THE TORSO: the seat body has no joint of its own (rigidly bolted to the rope's end, `swing_robot.xml`) -- its pose is purely and exactly a function of `swing_hinge`'s own angle, at a fixed, known radius (`ROPE_LENGTH=2.25m`). An IMU mounted there sees a clean single-axis, fixed-radius pendulum with zero kinematic contribution from lean/knee motion (rigid-body kinematics is exact regardless of what forces produce the motion) -- a strictly easier reconstruction problem than mounting on the torso, which would also pick up lean/knee-driven accelerations. Tested the easier, still-realistic-to-build case.

METHOD (`investigate_imu_angle_sensing.py`): added a MuJoCo `<site>` + `<accelerometer>`/`<gyro>` sensor pair to the seat body in a copy of `swing_robot.xml` (read-only, doesn't touch the file on disk) -- uses MuJoCo's own sensor physics for the synthetic readings rather than a hand-derived formula, verified against a controlled static test (fixed `theta`/`omega`, checked the accelerometer's Z-component against `g*cos(theta)` and the centripetal jump against `omega^2 * r` -- both matched exactly) before trusting it in a full dynamic rollout. Rolled out `best_model` deterministic for a full 240s episode, recording ground-truth `swing_angle` (qpos) alongside the synthetic sensor stream.

FINDING 1, gyro integration alone: excellent. 0.32 deg RMSE, 0.77 deg max error across the ENTIRE 240s episode (11,999 steps) -- confirmed this isn't a fluke by checking the error at the 60s mark (0.35 deg) and the 240s mark (0.82 deg) separately, both small and not blowing up. A gyro mounted rigidly to the seat measures `swing_hinge`'s own angular velocity directly and exactly (no other joint's motion can leak in, confirmed via the same rigid-kinematics argument above) -- integrating it from a known start (e.g. "at rest = 0 deg", a natural real-world calibration point) tracks the true angle almost perfectly over a single multi-minute session.

FINDING 2, accelerometer-based correction (for long-term drift beyond one session): did NOT work well in a first, naive attempt, and this is a real finding, not just an implementation gap to wave away. Isolating gravity's direction from the raw accelerometer reading requires subtracting the centripetal (`omega^2 * r`, validated correct via the static test) AND tangential (`r * dOmega/dt`) components -- the tangential term needs angular ACCELERATION, which this test estimated via simple finite-differencing of the gyro trace. That differencing amplifies even small high-frequency content in the gyro signal badly (dividing by `dt=0.02s` is a 50x noise gain) -- result: instantaneous accelerometer-only angle estimates were unreliable (~28 deg RMSE even during slow, low-centripetal moments near the turning points; ~64 deg RMSE near the fast bottom of the arc), and blending this into a standard 98/2 complementary filter made things WORSE than gyro alone (33 deg RMSE vs 0.32 deg), not better -- the correction signal was too noisy to help even at a small weight.

Reading: this doesn't mean accelerometer-based drift correction is impossible, it means naive per-step differencing isn't the right way to do it. A properly engineered version (heavier low-pass filtering on the derivative, a real Kalman/EKF filter with proper measurement-noise weighting, or -- likely the simplest practical fix -- only trusting the accelerometer during genuine REST moments, e.g. between sessions or natural pauses, rather than continuously) would plausibly work; not built or tested here.

PRACTICAL RECOMMENDATION: gyro-only integration, re-zeroed at rest, is very likely sufficient on its own given this policy's episodes run on a multi-minute timescale, not hours -- the observed drift (well under 1 deg over 240s) is small relative to `SPREAD_MAX_DEG`/`TARGET_BAND_HALF_WIDTH_DEG`-scale tolerances this project already works with. Revisit with a real filter only if actual sessions turn out to run much longer uninterrupted than this policy's own episodes do.

SIM-TO-REAL IMPLICATION, not yet acted on: `swing_env.py`'s observation currently gives the policy true, exact `swing_angle` (from `qpos`) during training -- a real deployment would feed it a gyro-integrated ESTIMATE instead, which this test shows is very close but not bit-exact. Training on perfect information while deploying on a slightly-noisy estimate is itself a sim-to-real gap domain randomization (roadmap item 3) doesn't automatically close, since that's about physical parameters, not observation noise. Worth considering injecting realistic angle-estimation error into the training observation directly (a cheap addition once domain randomization infrastructure exists) so the policy learns to work with the same quality of signal it'll actually have -- flagged here, not yet designed or implemented.

### 5.9v "Friction" reinterpreted as air drag, added to the sim, and swept for sensitivity -- resolves 5.9u's never-settles gap and gives domain randomization a real "resistance" axis

Follow-up to 5.9u: this sim's only energy-loss mechanism was joint damping, which isn't a plausible stand-in for air resistance (constant, not velocity-scaled) and is why passive decay never settled. Checked what MuJoCo contact friction is doing in this model first, since "friction" is the obvious other place resistance could live: nothing, confirmed by grep -- no geom friction/contype/conaffinity override anywhere, and the robot never contacts anything during normal operation, so that knob is entirely inert here. User's call: reinterpret "friction" as air drag instead of dropping the idea, since (a) it directly fixes 5.9u, (b) real hardware will have SOME control over it (shape/surface choices), asymmetrically -- easier to add drag than remove it below bare air -- and (c) it's worth knowing this model's sensitivity to it regardless.

Added via MuJoCo's built-in fluid model: `<option density="..." viscosity="..."/>`, both 0/inert by default (confirmed neither was previously set). Once nonzero, MuJoCo auto-computes drag per-body from each body's own inertia-implied ellipsoid -- no per-geom annotation needed for a first pass. Swept `density` only (`investigate_air_drag_sensitivity.py`), viscosity pinned at real air's 1.85e-5 Pa*s (negligible vs. the density-driven quadratic drag term at these speeds). Swept asymmetrically upward from real air (1.2 kg/m^3) per the user's own framing -- real hardware can't go below bare air, only above it.

Two things measured per density value, same anchor-substitution + compiled-model-verification discipline as every other sweep here (`model.opt.density` read back and checked against the intended value before trusting any result from it):

**Active-pumping ceiling** (hand-coded control law, 900s, same methodology as the mass/damping sweeps): 0.0 -> 71.79 deg, 0.6 -> 61.36 deg, 1.2 (real air) -> 52.88 deg, 2.4 -> 41.37 deg, 4.8 -> 25.23 deg, 9.6 -> 18.18 deg. Steady, continuous decline with no cliff -- unlike torso/head mass, this isn't a sharp threshold effect, it's drag directly taxing every pump cycle. Important caveat: even real-air density knocks the FIXED hand-coded law's ceiling to 52.88 deg, well under the 67.5 deg reward target -- this reflects that simple law's fixed technique, not a proven RL limit. The trained policy has never been asked to pump against any drag at all, so this number is a floor on what's achievable, not a ceiling on it.

**Passive-decay amplitude** (actual trained `best_model` policy pumps 200s, then lean/knee ctrl cut to neutral, amplitude measured at start and end of a 300s decay window): 0.0 -> 71.26 deg -> 66.43 deg (barely decays, confirms 5.9u's finding and the diagnosis behind it); 1.2 (real air) -> 61.34 deg -> 11.81 deg (real, plausible-looking decay -- a finer-grained trace at this density showed the expected fast-then-slow quadratic-drag shape: 64.65 deg at t=0.3s, 45.84 deg at t=25.7s, 20.57 deg at t=135.3s, 11.02 deg at t=297.5s); 2.4 -> 28.23 deg -> 6.05 deg (decays faster, but starts lower too -- same drag-naive policy pumps less effectively at 2x drag within the 200s window, consistent with the ceiling sweep above, not a separate effect).

Net result: air drag at real-air density alone fixes the never-settles gap with a realistic-looking decay curve, confirming this was the right fix for 5.9u. It also reintroduces the same tension seen with torso/head mass (5.9r): pushing the randomization range too far up trades realism for a regime the current fixed-technique numbers can't reach at all, though a policy retrained under drag should recover a meaningful chunk of that. Not yet written to `swing_robot.xml` on disk -- all testing so far is read-only against XML strings in the investigate script, matching this project's look-before-you-touch convention for exploratory work.

Clarified with the user afterward: `density` here is NOT modeling weather/altitude (real air only spans ~1.0-1.3 kg/m^3 sea level to a mountaintop, nowhere near the sweep's range) -- it's MuJoCo's only exposed drag knob, reused as a stand-in for the robot's OWN aerodynamic drag coefficient (shape, surface roughness, cross-section), not the air around it. "2x real air" means "as if the robot's own build were half as streamlined as bare air", not a claim about the weather.

### 5.9w Domain randomization implemented in the training pipeline

Combined all three sweeps (mass 5.9r, damping, air drag 5.9v) into concrete per-episode randomization ranges, added to `swing_env.py` as `DOMAIN_RANDOMIZATION_RANGES` (see that constant's own long comment for full per-parameter reasoning). Low-sensitivity parameters (rope/seat mass, all three joint dampings) use their full tested range as-is. Leg mass uses its full tested range except staying above 0.9 kg, where the hand-coded reference law itself went unstable (flagged in 5.9r as a control-law artifact, not physics). Two parameters needed an explicit user decision rather than just reading off the sweep data, since both sit next to a real performance wall rather than a smooth gradient:

- **Torso/head mass**: user chose to stay clear of both cliffs entirely (torso 2.5-3.0 kg, head 0.3-0.6 kg -- at or below current nominal, never above) rather than pin the cliffs down more precisely first. Revisit if a stronger lean servo is chosen later (open hardware decision, servos not yet sourced).
- **Air density**: user confirmed 2.4 kg/m^3 (2x real air, real air itself as the floor) as the upper bound, after the units clarification above resolved an initial "why would we need mountain-to-sea-floor range" concern -- the number was never about atmospheric conditions.

Implementation: `swing_robot.xml`'s text is read once at `SwingEnv.__init__` and kept alongside the compiled model. Every `reset()` call (when `DOMAIN_RANDOMIZATION_ENABLED = True`, which it now is) samples fresh values uniformly from each range, substitutes them into the XML text via the same unique-anchor + bounded-regex approach the investigate_*.py sweep scripts already established and verified, and recompiles a fresh `MjModel` from the result -- deliberately NOT direct post-compile mutation of `model.body_mass`/`dof_damping`/`opt.density`, since for mass specifically that would leave `body_inertia` inconsistent with the new value (MuJoCo doesn't recompute inertia tensors from a bare mass edit alone). Every recompiled model is verified before being trusted: joint/actuator counts checked against the baseline compile (structure shouldn't be able to change from a value-only substitution), total mass delta checked against the sum of intended per-parameter deltas, each damping value and `opt.density` read back directly. Physics stays fixed for the full episode once sampled -- only `reset()` re-randomizes, not mid-episode.

Smoke-tested directly: 8 episodes each produced a different mass/damping/density combination within the intended ranges, all verification asserts passed, and the wrapped `DummyVecEnv`/`VecNormalize` stack `train.py` actually uses ran cleanly on top of it. Per-episode recompile overhead measured at ~3ms -- negligible against a 360s (18,000-step) episode.

**IMPORTANT SCOPE CORRECTION caught before this was considered done:** the first pass made `DOMAIN_RANDOMIZATION_ENABLED` a straight module-level switch inside `swing_env.py`, which would have randomized physics in EVERY `SwingEnv()` instance across the whole project -- including `evaluate.py`. That's a real bug, not a style nit: `evaluate.py` is what `auto_train.py`'s entire gating stack (spread thresholds, streak detection, bootstrap-failure rate, `TARGET_OVERSHOOT_MAX`) reads, all of it calibrated assuming episode-to-episode variance means only policy stochasticity, never physics variance too. Randomizing eval silently breaks every one of those thresholds' meaning and makes them incomparable to the whole `evaluate_log.csv` history -- and separately, `investigate_air_drag_sensitivity.py`'s `passive_decay_amplitude()` swaps in a specific chosen density BEFORE calling `reset()`, which a global switch would have immediately overwritten.

Fixed before shipping: `domain_randomization` is now a per-instance constructor parameter on `SwingEnv`, defaulting OFF. Every existing call site (`evaluate.py`, `watch_policy.py`, `scan_checkpoints.py`, every `investigate_*.py`/scratch script) is completely unmodified and keeps getting fixed nominal physics, verified directly (`SwingEnv()._domain_randomization == False`, and confirmed those three files don't even mention the new parameter). Only `train.py`'s actual rollout-collection env (`vec_env`, the one PPO learns from) opts in via `env_kwargs=dict(domain_randomization=DOMAIN_RANDOMIZATION_ENABLED)`; its `eval_env` (best-model selection) and `prior_best_env` (resume-source comparison) deliberately stay off, so "best" and "target reached" keep meaning performance against the real nominal build. Verified directly: 4 resets of a training-style env produced 4 different total masses (7.23-8.96 kg), while 4 resets of an eval-style env produced the identical nominal 7.42 kg every time.

Not yet done: an actual training run under randomization hasn't happened yet -- `best_model`/`swing_policy.zip` still reflect the pre-randomization, single-nominal-geometry lineage. Expect a real performance dip when training resumes (the policy now has to generalize across the sampled range instead of overfitting one exact build), consistent with the air-drag ceiling sweep's own finding that even real-air drag alone costs a fixed-technique controller real height. Also not yet decided: whether `evaluate.py` should EVENTUALLY grow its own optional randomized-eval mode (to directly measure robustness, not just nominal performance) -- deliberately not done now, to avoid touching the gating logic in the same change that introduced randomized training.

### 5.9y First real round under domain randomization + observation noise: best_model improved, swing_policy showed a real (different) overshoot regression -- pick_resume_source() extended to catch it

User ran `auto_train.py` for real (not in this sandbox -- see 5.9w/5.9x for why that couldn't run to completion here). It stopped after exactly one round.

`best_model` came out of that round genuinely better: reward 11,372 (up from the ~9,690-9,910 range beforehand), peak angle 72.05 deg, spread 3.74 deg, 0 bootstrap failures, and ramp-up actually got FASTER (87.5s to 90% of steady state vs. 113-157s previously) -- no sign of the performance dip that was expected going in, at least not yet.

`swing_policy` (the raw round-end state) did not: peak angle 79-81 deg (det/sto), det_overshoot_penalty 12,089.63, sto_overshoot_penalty 7,305.06 -- both dramatically past every threshold. Spread stayed tight (3.87 deg) and bootstrap failures stayed at 0, meaning this ISN'T the unstable/inconsistent capture-basin pattern (5.9k) this project has been guarding against most recently -- it's a policy that pumps hard and consistently, just too high. That specific signature (tight spread + zero bootstrap failures + severe, consistent overshoot) matches the OLDER, already-solved `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` saga (swing_env.py, 0.3->0.6->1.2) far more closely than anything in this project's recent history.

Confirmed directly why `auto_train.py` stopped rather than guessing: `read_log()` combines each round by taking the WORSE of best_model's and swing_policy's numbers per metric (5.9i) -- worst overshoot here is swing_policy's 12,089.63, which trips `erratic_round()`'s `ERRATIC_OVERSHOOT_MAX` check (400) by more than 30x. Working exactly as designed.

**Real gap found and fixed before running another round:** `pick_resume_source()` (5.9k) only ever compared bootstrap-failure counts. Both rows here show 0 failures, so that check alone would have judged swing_policy "at least as reliable as best_model" and let the NEXT round resume from the overshooting state instead of the healthy one -- compounding a problem this mechanism exists specifically to prevent, and destroying any clean read on whether this was a one-round adaptation blip or something needing a real fix. Extended `pick_resume_source()` with a second, independent criterion: resume from best_model if swing_policy's overshoot penalty exceeds `RESUME_OVERSHOOT_MAX` (400, deliberately matching `auto_train.py`'s own `ERRATIC_OVERSHOOT_MAX` bar, kept as an independent local copy rather than imported, since train.py is invoked BY auto_train.py as a subprocess -- importing the other way would be backwards, and this project already keeps other constants like this as independent per-file copies rather than shared imports) while best_model's stays under it. Either criterion firing alone is now sufficient to trigger the best_model fallback.

Verified directly with four synthetic cases before trusting it against the real log: both clean -> unchanged (still resumes swing_policy); bootstrap regression only -> best_model (confirms the original 5.9k behavior wasn't broken by this change); overshoot regression only, using this round's own real numbers -> best_model (confirms the fix actually catches the incident that motivated it); both sides bad -> falls back to swing_policy (no clean fallback exists, same pre-existing edge-case behavior criterion 1 already had, not a new gap introduced here). Ran the real function against the actual current `evaluate_log.csv` afterward and confirmed it now correctly picks best_model with the overshoot reason -- the exact outcome this fix was for.

Deliberately NOT treated as proof `OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT` needs raising again -- this is one round of evidence under a brand-new training distribution (randomized physics + noisy angle observation), and this project's own history (5.9o -> 5.9p) is a direct lesson in not concluding "weight-driven" from a single round when a plausible alternative (early adaptation to a harder task) exists. The fix here is about not losing clean signal, not about pre-empting what that signal will show. Next step is simply running more rounds now that resuming is guaranteed to start from the healthy best_model, and watching whether swing_policy's overshoot resolves on its own or persists.

### 5.9z Four rounds in: best_model stable and healthy, swing_policy never converged -- auto_train.py's gating moved off swing_policy entirely, and a second, more serious bug caught before it could bite

Two more rounds ran after 5.9y's fix. Full picture across all four rounds under domain randomization + observation noise, best_model / swing_policy (det_overshoot_penalty) pairs: round 1 (11,372 / 12,089.6), round 2 (10,924 / 5,919.6), round 3 (10,701 / 10,883.4), round 4 (10,758 / 7,863.4). best_model stayed healthy and essentially flat all four times (0 bootstrap failures every round, reward always 10,701-11,372, comfortably above the pre-randomization ~9,690-9,910 baseline). swing_policy never converged -- no downward trend, and round 4 added a NEW symptom on top of the overshoot (angle spread jumped to 69.86 deg from a previously tight 3.4-5.6 deg band, plus swing_policy's first-ever bootstrap failure) -- if anything getting more erratic, not less.

Decided (user's call) that swing_policy's raw round-end state isn't worth continuing to gate on: it never gets trained on top of (pick_resume_source(), 5.9k/5.9y, already guarantees that every round), and under domain randomization it's an inherently noisier read than it used to be -- whatever the policy looked like right after a training batch of arbitrary sampled physics, evaluated cold against fixed nominal physics, not the stable best-of-the-round signal best_model is. Gating the whole auto_train.py loop's stop/continue decision on "worse of the two" (5.9i's original design) meant halting a demonstrably healthy pipeline every single round over noise in a metric that no longer determines what ships.

**Fix 1:** `read_log()` in `auto_train.py` now returns best_model's OWN numbers only -- not the worse-of-both combination. This is safe specifically because the risk 5.9i's combining existed to prevent (silently building further on a broken swing_policy) is now caught by an entirely different, more targeted mechanism (`pick_resume_source()`) that didn't exist yet when 5.9i was written. swing_policy's most recent row is still read (`latest_swing_policy_row()`, new) and printed every round for visibility -- a human watching still sees it -- it just can't halt the loop on its own anymore.

**Fix 2, a real bug caught before shipping, not a hypothetical:** running `streak_from_history()` against the newly best_model-only-gated but otherwise UNFILTERED full history came back with a streak of 29 -- best_model has rarely failed `round_passes` across dozens of rounds spanning many earlier, unrelated training regimes (pre-domain-randomization, different `ACTION_RATE_WEIGHT` phases, etc.). Since `CONSECUTIVE_HITS_REQUIRED` is only 3, seeding `consecutive_hits` at 29 means a SINGLE additional passing round -- even a lone new one -- would have immediately triggered "Target reached AND consistent, stopping" and permanently halted training. That's exactly the "one good round isn't trustworthy evidence" mistake `CONSECUTIVE_HITS_REQUIRED` itself exists to prevent, just smuggled in through stale, irrelevant history instead of a single lucky round -- and it would have fired on the very next check-in, silently declaring victory before domain randomization had actually been given a fair, uncontaminated trial.

Fixed with `DOMAIN_RANDOMIZATION_START_TIMESTAMP` (the exact timestamp of the first post-randomization best_model row) -- every gating check (`read_log()`, and therefore streak/cliff/decline/stall, all of which consume its output) now only ever sees rows from that boundary onward. Re-ran the same check afterward: streak against the properly-scoped 4-row history comes back as 4, not 29 -- and since all 4 of those rows are genuine, uncontaminated evidence gathered specifically under the current regime, that streak already meets the project's own trustworthiness bar (3). Unlike the unfiltered 29, this is legitimate: if the next round also passes, a "target reached" declaration at that point would be fair, not premature.

Verified directly, not assumed: reloaded `auto_train.py` and called `read_log()` / `latest_swing_policy_row()` / `streak_from_history()` / `single_round_cliff()` / `erratic_round()` / `declining_trend()` against the real, current `evaluate_log.csv` -- confirmed exactly 4 gating rows (matching the 4 real rounds), all passing, streak=4, no cliff/erratic/decline flags, and `latest_swing_policy_row()` correctly surfacing round 4's concerning numbers (79.49 deg, overshoot 7,863.4, spread 69.86, 1 bootstrap failure) for print-only visibility. Also confirmed the file still imports and compiles cleanly.

**CLOSING NOTE, confirmed by the user:** a 5th round ran clean (72.06 deg, overshoot 1.73, spread 4.41, 0 bootstrap failures -- best_model's 5th in a row), pushing the properly-boundary-scoped streak to 5, past `CONSECUTIVE_HITS_REQUIRED` (3). `auto_train.py` printed "Target reached AND consistent" and stopped itself, exactly the outcome `DOMAIN_RANDOMIZATION_START_TIMESTAMP`'s fix was built to make trustworthy when it eventually happened, rather than premature. This is real confirmation domain randomization (5.9w) + observation noise (5.9x) training works: `best_model` held target angle, low overshoot, tight spread, and zero bootstrap failures across 5 consecutive rounds trained under randomized mass/damping/drag and gyro-drift-noised angle observation. Roadmap item 3 (domain randomization) is DONE, not just implemented.

swing_policy's own round-end state never resolved across those 5 rounds (worst on the final round: 93.24 deg peak, steady-state 89.87 deg, overshoot 12,655.5 -- highest of any round) -- read as a likely-expected characteristic of evaluating an arbitrary mid-training snapshot against fixed nominal physics under a training signal now deliberately spread across a harder distribution, not a bug, given it never gets deployed or trained from again (`pick_resume_source()` guarantees that). Not proven with certainty -- flagged as the one open judgment call from this whole push, in case it matters for justifying decisions to other parties later. Not acted on further.

## 6. Tooling quick reference

- **Starting fresh after a structural model change** (like the knee swap, or the torso/seat geometry fix): `python archive_old_model.py` — it prints what it's about to move (checkpoint count, step range, best angle seen) and asks for `yes` before doing anything, so double check that summary actually matches what you intend to archive before confirming. Then `python train.py`. Confirm it prints "starting a fresh random policy" behavior, not "continuing training" — if it says it found an existing `swing_policy.zip`, the archive step didn't run or didn't find what it expected.
- **Routine check-in:** `python auto_train.py` — runs up to 3 rounds, stops itself per section 5.4, safe to re-run repeatedly as time allows. Each round now evaluates and logs both `best_model` and `swing_policy` (round-end state) automatically (5.9i) — the printed "combined result" is the worse of the two, not just `best_model`'s number.
- **Manually check a specific checkpoint:** set `EXTRA_MODEL_PATH`/`EXTRA_VECNORM_PATH` at the top of `evaluate.py` (NOT `MODEL_PATH`/`VECNORM_PATH` — those are now the fixed `best_model` pairing, always checked), run it, **remember to set both back to `None` before running `auto_train.py` again** (it runs `evaluate.py` as-is, and a lingering third check will keep running and logging every round).
- **After a decline/cliff/stall stop — full checkpoint-rescue checklist:**
  1. `python scan_checkpoints.py` (incremental — only tests new files). Read the end of its output: it now auto-detects the longest well-supported streak across resume-session lineages (section 5.6) and prints a recommended checkpoint plus copy-paste-ready commands for the next two steps.
  2. Copy the checkpoint/vecnorm paths it prints into `EXTRA_MODEL_PATH`/`EXTRA_VECNORM_PATH` at the top of `evaluate.py` (5.9i — `MODEL_PATH`/`VECNORM_PATH` are now fixed to the `best_model` pairing, don't edit those).
  3. `python evaluate.py` — it'll print best_model's and swing_policy's usual reports plus a third one for the candidate; check the candidate's 15-episode deterministic angle is close to what the scan showed, spread is small (a few degrees, not tens), overshoot ~0.
  4. If it holds up: `python promote_checkpoint.py <path-to-checkpoint.zip>` (the exact command is also printed by `scan_checkpoints.py`).
  5. **Set `EXTRA_MODEL_PATH`/`EXTRA_VECNORM_PATH` back to `None`** before running `auto_train.py` again — it runs `evaluate.py` as-is, and won't catch that you left a third check pointed somewhere else.
  6. `python auto_train.py` to resume the normal loop.
- **Visual sanity check:** `mjpython watch_policy.py` (note: `mjpython`, not `python`, on macOS). Real-time paced — give it a minute or two before judging, since pumping builds up gradually.

### 5.9x Gyro-derived swing-angle observation noise, anticipating the real sensing gap flagged in 5.9t

Follow-up to 5.9t: training has always given the policy exact, `qpos`-based `swing_angle`, but real deployment will feed it a gyro-integrated estimate instead (no direct angle sensor is practical). 5.9t flagged this as a real sim-to-real gap and left it unactioned; this closes it by injecting noise into training that mimics the actual measured error.

Added to `swing_env.py`: `swing_angle` only (not velocity, not lean/knee) gets a random-WALK bias, re-zeroed every episode (matching the deployment plan's gyro re-zero on entering rest, 5.9u), NOT i.i.d. per-step noise -- gyro-integration error compounds over time, it isn't independent per sample, so a random walk is the physically right shape. Calibrated directly from 5.9t's own measurement (0.32 deg RMSE over a 240s/12,000-step episode) as a genuine per-step drift-RATE constant, not a re-fit number -- applying the same rate over this project's actual 360s/18,000-step episodes naturally gives more total drift (~0.39 deg expected final std), which is correct, not an inconsistency. velocity/lean/knee stay untouched since 5.9t only investigated and measured the angle-reconstruction gap, not those.

Same caveat as 5.9t's own: 5.9t's number came from MuJoCo's noise-free synthetic gyro, so this is a floor on real drift, not a ceiling -- real MEMS sensor noise on top of pure integration error isn't modeled, for lack of any real hardware to characterize it against yet.

**Correctness point verified directly, not assumed:** noise must only ever reach the array returned to the policy, never the reward/termination computation, which has to stay grounded in the real physical angle (a training-time proxy target, not something the robot itself computes onboard). Confirmed by running the same seed, same fixed action sequence, same physics through two envs differing ONLY in `observation_noise` -- resulting reward arrays were bit-identical (`max abs diff: 0.0`) across 500 steps. Also confirmed: with noise on, the returned observation genuinely differs from ground truth (max ~0.11 deg over a 2000-step run); with everything at default (evaluate.py's config), returned angle matches ground truth exactly, 0.0 diff over 200 steps.

Wired into `train.py` exactly like domain randomization (5.9w) -- `observation_noise=True` only on the rollout-collection env, left at its default-off everywhere else (`eval_env`, `prior_best_env`, `evaluate.py`, `watch_policy.py`, `scan_checkpoints.py`, every investigate script), for the same reason: "best model" and "target reached" need to keep meaning performance with exact angle feedback against the real nominal build, matching every historical `evaluate_log.csv` threshold. Whether `evaluate.py` should eventually grow its own optional noisy-eval mode is an open question, same framing as 5.9w's domain-randomization eval question -- not resolved here.

Training under BOTH domain randomization and this observation noise together has since been run for real (5.9y/5.9z) and confirmed working: `best_model` passed 5 consecutive rounds cleanly, and `auto_train.py` itself printed "Target reached AND consistent" and stopped on its own -- see 5.9z's closing note.

**Deployment environment, confirmed by the user (not assumed):** indoor only, never outdoor -- no weatherproofing/temperature-extreme/UV consideration needed for any hardware choice. ### 5.9aa Torso/head mass reduction as a lean-servo cost lever -- the inverse question from 5.9r's mass sweep

User's framing: torso+head mass is a variable they control (unlike, say, real-world air resistance), and it's the specific thing 5.9r found taxes the lean/torso servo's torque budget hardest. Question: how much torque could actually be saved by intentionally building a lighter torso/head assembly, as a way to justify a cheaper/smaller lean servo?

5.9r asked "given FIXED torque, how does the ceiling change with mass" (found the cliff). This is the inverse: "given a TARGET ceiling (67.5 deg, the actual reward target, not a looser number), what's the minimum torque needed at a given (reduced) torso+head mass" -- built as `investigate_torque_savings.py`, same hand-coded-law + anchor-substitution + compiled-model-verification methodology as every other sweep here, extended to also substitute `lean_motor`'s `forcerange` (new anchor, verified against `model.actuator_forcerange`). Grid-swept torque in 0.5 Nm steps from 3.0 to 8.1 Nm (8.1 = current baseline) across three scenario sets: torso+head reduced together (the user's actual question), torso alone (head held at baseline), and head alone (torso held at baseline) -- the latter two to attribute how much of the combined effect each one contributes.

**Combined reduction (torso and head cut by the same %):**

| Reduction | Torso (kg) | Head (kg) | Min. lean torque for 67.5 deg |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% (baseline) | 3.00 | 0.40 | 7.5 N·m |
| 10% | 2.70 | 0.36 | 7.0 N·m |
| 20% | 2.40 | 0.32 | 6.0 N·m |
| 30% | 2.10 | 0.28 | 5.5 N·m |
| 40% | 1.80 | 0.24 | 4.5 N·m |
| 50% | 1.50 | 0.20 | 4.0 N·m |

Real, substantial lever: cutting torso+head mass in half roughly halves the torque needed too (7.5 -> 4.0 N·m).

**Attribution (which one actually matters):** torso dominates by a wide margin. Cutting torso alone by 50% (head held at baseline) took torque from 7.5 -> 4.5 N·m (3.0 N·m saved, removing 1.5 kg). Cutting head alone by 50% (torso held at baseline) only took it from 7.5 -> 6.5 N·m (1.0 N·m saved, removing 0.2 kg) -- and most of that head-alone curve was flat (7.5 N·m unchanged through a 10% head cut, still 7.0 N·m through a 40% cut). Makes sense given the baseline masses (torso at 3.0 kg vs. head at 0.4 kg, 7.5x more mass to work with) -- but worth having confirmed rather than assumed, since it means design effort aimed at lightening the head specifically has a hard, low ceiling on how much torque it can ever buy back, while torso has real room.

**One incidental finding, not the main point:** at the CURRENT baseline mass (3.0/0.4 kg, 0% reduction), the minimum torque clearing 67.5 deg came back as 7.5 N·m, not the full 8.1 N·m placeholder -- meaning the original 8.1 N·m choice already had about 0.6 N·m of built-in margin above the bare minimum for hitting the reward target specifically. Not the same claim as `swing_robot.xml`'s original "~8 Nm threshold below which it can't pump at all" comment (that was calibrated to a stricter bootstrap-from-rest capability, and this sweep's own seed-kick-based ceiling extraction implicitly tests that too -- see the sharp failure at 3.0-3.5 N·m in the -50% row, ceiling collapsing to ~4-5 deg) -- just noting the two numbers are close but not identical questions, so don't conflate them if this comes up again.

Same limitation as every hand-coded-law sweep in this project: a physics-only proxy under a fixed simple control law, not a measurement of what a retrained RL policy could do at each torque/mass combination -- retraining per grid cell was far too expensive to run for real. Useful for picking a servo torque CLASS to shop for, not a guarantee of the exact number.

### 5.9ad Per-leg "organic" variation at deployment time: how much can the trained policy tolerate? (`investigate_leg_variation.py`)

User idea: the two real knee servos are fully independent, so give each a slightly different, slowly drifting version of the single knee command the policy outputs (position, timing, or speed), for a more organic look -- as deployment-time post-processing on the Pi, NO retraining. The sim has one knee hinge standing in for both legs, so left/right can't be simulated directly; the script uses the first-order stand-in that two equal independent legs move the combined leg mass like one leg commanded the AVERAGE of the two. Mirrored position offsets (+d/-d) average to zero, so they're benign to first order by construction (second-order joint nonlinearity not modeled). Tested the harder, non-mirrored cases on `best_model` (3 episodes per scenario, nominal physics, slow AR(1) drift with ~5 s correlation time):

- **Position offset of the average command, RMS 2/5/10/20 deg:** no meaningful effect (steady-state ~70 deg throughout, vs. 70.5 baseline; peak 72-74.5). Even 20 deg of unmirrored drift is tolerated.
- **Independent drift per leg, 5/10/20 deg each:** same -- steady 70-71, no degradation.
- **Timing lag on one leg (other leg unlagged), in 0.02 s control steps:** 2 steps (40 ms) is fine (steady 73, slight rise); 5 steps (100 ms) overshoots badly (peak 77.7, overshoot penalty ~10000, reward ~-500); 10 steps worse (peak 80.6); 25 steps oddly back near normal (non-monotonic -- likely the lag passing through a half-period, not a safe region). Timing is the sensitive axis, as suspected: the lag makes the swing pump HIGHER than target, not fail.

Practical limits: position variation of several degrees (even 10+) is safe and enough for a visible effect; keep any per-leg timing offset to ~2 steps (40 ms) or less; speed variation not tested here and the knee is already near the servo's speed ceiling (see velocity discussion), so avoid slowing a leg. Caveat: 3 episodes per scenario is a rough read, and the real two-leg effect is unverified until tested on hardware.

### 5.9af Pi control code (`pi/`), written and tested against the sim only -- NOT yet run on hardware

Files: `pi/export_policy.py` (SB3 -> `policy.npz`, numpy-only on the Pi; verified identical to SB3 to 5e-7), `pi/config.py` (every hardware number: IDs, signs, limits, torque limits, drift, safety), `pi/swing_core.py` (conversions, gyro-integrating angle estimator with trapezoid rule, policy, per-knee slow drift, bootstrap, rest detector), `pi/hardware.py` (servo bus via `feetech-servo-sdk` packet layer + STS registers, MPU-6050), `pi/run_swing.py` (the cycle), `pi/servo_check.py`, `pi/servo_step_test.py`, `pi/sim_test.py` (runs the exact Pi logic against MuJoCo), `pi/SETUP.md` (bring-up order). Joint mapping: `raw = 2048 - 11.378 * sim_deg` for lean AND both knees (as-built, both flipped); control rate 50 Hz.

Findings from the sim tests, all affecting design:
- **Joint velocity smoothing hurts.** Finite-difference joint velocity with EMA 0.5 raised the peak 73 -> 77 deg; plain differencing (EMA 1.0) reproduces the exact-observation result (73.2). `JOINT_VEL_EMA = 1.0`.
- **The gyro bias must be good.** Residual bias after calibration: 0.05 deg/s -> still swings but ~9 deg angle error after 200 s (peak 81); 0.2 deg/s -> 37 deg error, policy collapses (peak 28). Hence the re-zero at every rest, a warm-up, and acceptance thresholds in `pi/mpu6050_check.py drift` (the script moved into `pi/`; it also has a `rock` mode for the axis/sign check) (<0.02 good, <0.06 marginal).
- **The policy stalls from dead rest.** With the sim's random disturbances off (hardware has none), zero initial velocity leaves the swing stuck at ~4 deg indefinitely; seed kick 0.08 rad/s eventually starts (72 deg by 180 s); earlier "all kicks bootstrap" results had the random disturbance kicks on, which hid this. Lean/knee pulses did not help. A self-excited start (lean follows the swing rate through a 0.5 deg/s Schmitt trigger, amplitude 0.6, handover to the policy at 10 deg) works from dead rest: handover at ~47 s, peak 72.7 with drift on. Hysteresis is sensitive (1 dps worked, 2.9 dps did not start), so it is kept small. If the gyro sign is wrong this start does not grow and times out at 150 s with a message naming GYRO_SIGN -- a built-in sign check.
- Build-up time from a tiny kick varies a lot (about 1.5 to >5 min across seeds) before reaching ~75 deg.
- Passive settling in the sim takes > 30 minutes (no meaningful drag in this sim; README 5.9u/5.9v) -- real settle time unknown; the cycle just waits (with periodic status) until the gyro rate has been < 20 deg/s for 12 s and then fully quiet (std < 0.5 deg/s) before re-zeroing.
- Complete with drift on (up to +-5 deg per knee), a constant 0.05 deg/s gyro bias, quantized servo counts: peak 72.7, steady ~72.

Design decisions from the user: runs start immediately (no hand push); cycle = ACTIVE (build-up, then HOLD_SECONDS near target; originally written as a flat 360 s, corrected below) -> PASSIVE (zero pose, torque held) -> re-zero -> repeat; errors also go PASSIVE and the cycle restarts (3 quick faults in a row or a dead bus relaxes the servos and exits); Ctrl+C relaxes immediately. Auto-start at power-up is deferred.

**Hold time (user clarification):** `HOLD_SECONDS` (config.py, placeholder 240) is the time spent NEAR the target swing -- not pushing higher, not letting it fall -- not total run time. The run goes BOOT -> build-up -> holds until the windowed peak (last 5 s) has been >= `HOLD_MIN_DEG` (55) for `HOLD_SECONDS` in total, then PASSIVE; `ACTIVE_MAX_SECONDS` (900) caps build-up + hold and a miss is treated as a fault. Sim check: 60 s hold, target reached ~t=99 s, hold complete at t=159 s. The user will tune `HOLD_SECONDS`.

**OPEN, late in the project: real wind/air drag.** The user notes it will be significant and has no value or range yet. What exists already: training randomizes air density over 1.2-2.4 kg/m^3 (1.2 = still air; 5.9w/`swing_env.py` DR table), and 5.9v swept the hand-coded law over 0-9.6 (ceiling falls 72 -> 18 deg) and measured passive decay at 0, 1.2, 2.4 (1.2: 61 -> 12 deg in 300 s). The trained policy's own performance above ~2.4 is untested. The sim's drag is a placeholder and passive settling in the sim takes > 30 min, so the real settle time, the real pump effort needed (more drag = more energy to hold the target), and the policy's robustness to it are all unknown until measured on the real swing. Plan when the swing exists: measure free-decay (release from a known amplitude, torque held at the zero pose, log the amplitude vs time from `logs/` / the gyro), fit a drag coefficient, then re-run the 5.9v drag sensitivity sweep over a range bracketing it and widen domain randomization if needed.

Reading: the whole 2.0-2.5 m range works, and works at about the trained amplitude (74 -> 71 deg); a longer rope builds up more slowly and settles lower, a shorter one faster and higher. The policy keeps working to about 1.9 m (shorter ropes overshoot the 75 deg cap: 76.6 at 1.75, 78.6 at 1.6; at 1.5 it fails to build) and to about 3.0 m (amplitude 65.5, still above the 62.5 target, but 4 min to build). At 3.5 m and 4.0 m it never starts -- that is the self-excited START routine (tuned at 2.25) failing to reach its 10 deg handover, not necessarily the policy; the policy alone at those lengths was not tested. Caveats: one seed per length; real rope also changes the 0.5 kg mass and stretch; perfect gyro bias assumed.

### 5.9ah Do per-element mass limits combine? Whole-robot combinations (`investigate_mass_combinations.py`)

Earlier mass sweeps (5.9r) varied one element at a time. Here the deployed pipeline (`pi/`, dead-rest start, disturbances off, drift on, air density 1.2, damping nominal, rope 2.25 m, 300 s, one seed per row) was run on the 32 corners of the 5-element box (rope 0.25-1.0, seat 0.75-2.5, leg 1.0-2.5, torso 2.5-3.0, head 0.3-0.6 kg -- the domain-randomization ranges), the midpoint, an inner box, and random points. Amplitude = mean 5 s-peak over the last 100 s (target 62.5, "good" >= ~58).

- **Sums of extremes do not work as a rule.** All-minimum (4.80 kg): 62.0 deg, fine. All-maximum (9.60 kg): 34.6, fails. Midpoint (7.20 kg): 64.0, fine. But total mass is not what decides it: 9.30 kg (c30) reaches 59.5 while 5.60 kg (c03) fails at 20.4.
- **What decides it is the upper-body / leg balance (torso + head vs leg); rope and seat barely matter.** Mean amplitude by corner group (rope/seat varied inside each): upper body 2.8 kg + leg 1.0: 61.6; 3.1 + 1.0: 70.6; 3.3 + 1.0: 68.7; **3.6 + 1.0: 21.7 (fails, all four rope/seat variants)**; 2.8 + 2.5: 48.8 (marginal); 3.1 + 2.5: 60.2; 3.3 + 2.5: 58.8; **3.6 + 2.5: 42.8 (marginal/fails)**. I.e. the corners to avoid are torso 3.0 + head 0.6 together (the lean servo's 8.1 N*m budget, as 5.9r suspected) with either the lightest or the heaviest leg, and the lightest upper body (2.5 + 0.3) with the heaviest leg.
- **Aiming for the middle of each range works, with margin.** The inner box (torso, head, leg each at the 25% and 75% points of their ranges, rope and seat at mid; 8 corners) all reached the target: 60.2-69.0 deg, totals 6.6-7.8 kg. Random points near the middle also fine (59.9-70.5); one random point (r1: torso 2.97, head 0.49, leg 1.19, total 6.47) reached only 53.2 -- close to the torso+head-heavy/light-leg failure corner.
- **Found along the way: the start routine could stall.** Five corners (all with a light 1.0 kg leg and heavy torso/head) never moved at all (max 0.2 deg): the lean followed a swing rate that never crossed the 0.5 deg/s threshold. Added `BOOT_STALL_S = 3` (flip the lean after 3 s without a crossing); 3 of the 5 then started and built normally (68-71 deg), the other 2 (heaviest upper body + light leg) started but the policy itself did not build (21 deg). The earlier tests (rope length, 2.25 m midpoint, `sim_test.py`) are unchanged by this.
- Caveats: one seed per point; the 5 masses are the only things varied (centers of mass, lever arms, servo speed/lag under load are not); the policy was trained with each element sampled independently over this box, so these corners are inside its training distribution -- at corners it has simply seen less often.

**IMU bench results (user's board):** WHO_AM_I 0x68; gyro bias steady between runs to ~0.02 deg/s; 60 s drift 0.017 deg on the hinge axis (worst axis 0.004 deg/s -> good); accelerometer scale correct (~0.996 g from a flip) with a large fixed Z offset of about -0.2 g (irrelevant: the policy uses only the gyro); `rock` test (front edge up then back): Y integrated +38.9 deg with X/Z within ~2 deg, so `GYRO_AXIS="y"`, `GYRO_SIGN=+1` confirmed (the final reading caught the return in progress, not a scale problem, per the user).

### 5.9ai Real servo step test (unloaded) vs the sim, and the slew-limit sim check

Bench result (STS3095, 7.5 V, config torque caps 870 lean / 290 knee, `SERVO_SPEED=3000`, `SERVO_ACC=150`, unloaded): knee 0 -> 60 deg: 90% in ~1026 ms, peak speed ~98-101 deg/s; lean 0 -> 40 deg: 90% in 488 ms (513 ms back), peak 164 deg/s. Cause not established: the torque cap may itself be throttling speed (the lean at 87% torque is fastest; the knee at 29% is slowest) -- untested; `servo_step_test.py <joint> <a> <b> <torque>` now takes an optional torque override to check.

Not yet tested: the real response with the full mass attached (several days away, per the user), which will be slower still; and any acceleration limit or delay in the real servo.

## 8. Glossary

Terms used throughout this doc without re-explaining every time, grouped by where they come from rather than alphabetically, since the grouping is itself useful context. Not exhaustive — just the recurring/load-bearing ones.

**Reinforcement learning / PPO**

- **Policy** — the neural network being trained; maps an observation (current swing/lean/knee angles and velocities) to an action (target lean/knee positions). "Policy" and "model" are used interchangeably here.
- **PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)** — the RL algorithm used (via Stable-Baselines3) to train the policy. Its internals show up in diagnostics below (`approx_kl`, `clip_range`, `value_loss`, `target_kl`).
- **Episode** — one full simulated run (`episode_seconds=240` in `swing_env.py`, ~12,000 environment steps), ending by time truncation rather than an explicit failure condition.
- **Reward** / **episode return** — reward is the per-step number the policy is trained to maximize (see `_height_reward()` and the overshoot-penalty terms, section 5.5a); episode return is reward summed over a whole episode, what `evaluate.py` reports as "reward" per episode.
- **Deterministic vs. stochastic evaluation** — deterministic runs the policy's mean action, no exploration noise (what `watch_policy.py` and real deployment would use); stochastic samples from the action distribution the way training rollouts do. `evaluate.py` reports both — a large gap between them means the policy hasn't yet converged to a reliable mean behavior.
- **Reward shaping** — designing the reward function itself, as opposed to the algorithm or hyperparameters, to steer behavior. Most of section 5.5a is reward-shaping work.
- **Checkpoint** — a saved snapshot of policy weights (+ paired `VecNormalize` stats) at a point in training, saved periodically into `checkpoints/` by `CheckpointCallback`.
- **Promotion** — copying a chosen checkpoint into both `swing_policy.zip` and `best_model/best_model.zip` (via `promote_checkpoint.py`), making it the new resume point and reported best. See section 4.
- **Gamma** — PPO's discount factor (how much future reward is weighted vs. immediate reward). Lowered over this project (0.9995 → 0.999 → 0.997) to reduce value-function variance; see 5.5.
- **Value function / `value_loss` / `explained_variance`** — PPO trains an auxiliary network to predict expected future return; `value_loss` is its training error, `explained_variance` how well it's currently doing. Spikes/crashes in these were the diagnostic signature behind 5.5a-iii.
- **GAE (Generalized Advantage Estimation) / advantage** — how PPO estimates "how much better was this action than expected," computed from the value function — why a corrupted value function can corrupt the policy update too (5.5a-iii).
- **`approx_kl` / `target_kl`** — how much the policy changed in one update; `target_kl` is a circuit breaker that stops a batch's gradient epochs early if that change gets too large (5.5).
- **`clip_range` / `clip_fraction` / `clip_range_vf`** — PPO limits how far a single update can move the policy (`clip_range`); `clip_fraction` is how often that limit is actually hit. `clip_range_vf` is the analogous limit for the value function specifically (tried and reverted in 5.5a-iii).
- **`VecNormalize` / reward normalization** — a Stable-Baselines3 wrapper that can normalize observations and/or rewards using running statistics. Reward normalization interacted badly with this project's episode-return volatility and was turned off (5.5); observation normalization is still on.
- **`EvalCallback` / `CheckpointCallback`** — Stable-Baselines3 training hooks. `EvalCallback` periodically tests the policy and updates `best_model/` on improvement (see 5.3 for a bug in its own rigor); `CheckpointCallback` just saves on a fixed step interval.
- **Observation space / action space** — the shape and bounds of what the policy sees (angles/velocities) and outputs (target joint positions), defined in `swing_env.py`.

**Physics / simulation**

- **MuJoCo** — the physics engine simulating the swing; `swing_robot.xml` defines the model, `mujoco` is the Python binding used throughout.
- **Gymnasium** — the environment API standard `SwingEnv` (`swing_env.py`) implements (`reset()`/`step()`/etc.).
- **Hinge joint / prismatic joint** — a hinge rotates about an axis (all three joints in the current model — `swing_hinge`, `lean_hinge`, `knee_hinge`); a prismatic joint slides linearly (the old sliding-counterweight joint, replaced — section 2).
- **Actuator / PD servo** — what drives a joint. This project uses MuJoCo `position` actuators (PD/servo-style: hold a commanded position, correcting automatically), not raw torque motors — see `swing_robot.xml`'s actuator comments.
- **`qpos` / `qvel`** — MuJoCo's per-joint position and velocity state arrays; what the observation is built from.

**Project-specific shorthand**

- **Peak angle (`peak_angle_deg`)** — the single highest swing angle reached anywhere in an episode. `evaluate.py`'s original headline metric — found to be a misleading proxy on its own (5.5a-ix/x), since one rare excursion can dominate it regardless of how good the rest of the episode was.
- **Spread** — (max − min) of peak angle across a batch of evaluation episodes (usually 5). Catches a policy that looks fine on average but is actually inconsistent seed-to-seed; see `evaluate.py` and the "erratic" check in 5.4.
- **Overshoot** — swinging past `TARGET_ANGLE_DEG`. `overshoot_deg` is how far past; `overshoot_penalty` is the resulting reward penalty — several distinct mechanisms feed into this now, the subject of most of the 5.5a saga.
- **Time-in-band / wall touches** — `time_in_band_frac` and `wall_touch_count`, `evaluate.py` metrics (5.5a-x) meant to see past peak angle's blind spot: what fraction of an episode looked like target behavior, and how many distinct times it actually reached near the physical joint limit. Wall touches also now feed the reward directly (`WALL_TOUCH_PENALTY`, 5.5a-xii), not just the diagnostic — a fixed cost per distinct touch, so repeated bouncing off the limit within one episode is discouraged, not just the worst single excursion.
- **Seed kick / disturbance kick** — two different randomized velocity perturbations in `swing_env.py`. A seed kick happens once per `reset()` (breaks perfect symmetry so the policy has something to react to — 5.1); a disturbance kick can happen at any step mid-episode (`DISTURBANCE_PROB_PER_STEP`), simulating something like a gust — what phase 2 (section 2) trains the policy to reject.
- **Cold start** — the early-episode period before pumping "catches," reacting to and amplifying the seed kick; expected/accepted noisy behavior, not a bug (5.1).
- **Collapse** — this project's term for a policy's performance suddenly dropping to near-zero, sometimes recovering, sometimes not for a long time. Subject of most of section 5.5.
- **Cliff / decline / stall / erratic** — `auto_train.py`'s four failure-shaped stopping conditions (section 5.4): a cliff is a sudden one-round drop, a decline is a gradual multi-round slide, a stall is a flat plateau, and erratic is a single round with high internal spread/overshoot despite a normal-looking mean.
- **Streak** — `scan_checkpoints.py`'s term for a run of consecutive checkpoints all falling within a "good" angle band (`STREAK_GOOD_MIN_DEG`/`MAX_DEG`), used to recommend a checkpoint that's reliably good rather than a lucky single reading.
- **Target angle** — the angle the reward is shaped around (`TARGET_ANGLE_DEG` in `swing_env.py`); currently 67.5°, redefined from 80° in 5.5a-xi. Also exists as a separate, hand-duplicated constant in `auto_train.py` for its own "reached" gate — the two aren't linked in code, worth remembering if one changes without the other.
- **Height reward / height cap / decay / taper** — the core reward term: `arm_length * (1 - cos(angle))` below target, eased near the cap (taper), then decaying past it down to a fixed floor rather than staying flat (decay). The full shape lives in `_height_reward()` (`swing_env.py`), built up piece by piece across 5.5a-v, vii, and viii.
- **Budget (episode overshoot penalty budget)** — `EPISODE_OVERSHOOT_PENALTY_CAP`, a per-episode cap on cumulative overshoot penalty so a single bad episode can't produce an unbounded outlier return (5.5a-iii).
- **Phase-lock / resonant pumping** — the working hypothesis that effective swing-pumping depends on hitting the right timing relative to the swing's own natural oscillation, and pumping badly out of phase can actively cancel motion rather than just fail to add it (5.5, 5.5a-x).
